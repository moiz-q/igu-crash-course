When we don't know exactly what happened, we **guess from the evidence**. Detectives, scientists and ordinary people do this all the time: *"He **must have missed** the bus."* *"She **can't have seen** the message."* These are **past modals of deduction**.


@widget viz name=en-certainty

## Structure

**modal + have + past participle (V3)**

| How sure are you? | Modal | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~95% sure it **happened** | **must have** + V3 | *The ground is wet. It **must have rained** last night.* |
| about 50% — possible | **might have / may have / could have** + V3 | *He's late. He **might have missed** the bus.* |
| ~95% sure it **did not** happen | **can't have / couldn't have** + V3 | *She **can't have stolen** it — she was in Ankara.* |
| 50% it didn't happen | **might not have / may not have** + V3 | *They **might not have heard** the alarm.* |

**Important:**

- The opposite of *must have* is **can't have** — **not** *mustn't have*.
- *could have* has two meanings: possibility (*He could have left already*) and an ability that was not used (*You could have called me!* = you didn't).
- In speaking, *must have* sounds like "must-of" — but always **write** *have*.

## Present vs. past

| Present guess | Past guess |
| --- | --- |
| He **must be** tired. | He **must have been** tired. |
| She **might be** at home. | She **might have been** at home. |
| It **can't be** true. | It **can't have been** true. |

## Worked example: the empty lighthouse

> In 1900, three keepers disappeared from a lighthouse on a small Scottish island. Passing ships noticed that the lamp was not lit. When a boat finally arrived, the keepers were gone, and two of the three waterproof coats were missing.

- Two coats were missing, so two men **must have gone** outside in bad weather.
- The third coat was still there, so the third man **might have run** out in a hurry.
- The weather had been terrible for days. A big wave **could have washed** them into the sea.
- They **can't have left** by boat — no boat had come to the island.

## Practice (18 items)

Complete with **must have / might have / can't have** + the verb.

_ The windows are broken and the TV is gone. Someone ___ (break) in. = must have broken in
_ I can't find my keys. I ___ (leave) them at work — I'm not sure. = might have left | may have left | could have left
_ He ___ (write) this essay alone; it's far too perfect for a first-year student. = can't have written | couldn't have written
_ She passed with 98%. She ___ (study) very hard. = must have studied
_ The lights are off. They ___ (go) to bed already. = might have gone | must have gone | may have gone
_ You ___ (see) Ali yesterday — he's been in Germany all month! = can't have seen | couldn't have seen
_ The ground is white. It ___ (snow) during the night. = must have snowed
_ The explorers ___ (survive) for weeks without food — maybe they found fish. = might have survived | could have survived | may have survived
_ Nobody answered. They ___ (hear) the bell. = can't have heard | might not have heard | didn't hear
_ The ship disappeared without any radio message. A sudden storm ___ (sink) it. = might have sunk | could have sunk | may have sunk
_ My phone was on the table a minute ago. Someone ___ (take) it! = must have taken
_ They ___ (be) very hungry after walking 30 km. = must have been
_ He ___ (be) at the party — he was ill in bed. = can't have been | couldn't have been
_ Why didn't you tell me? You ___ (call) me! (ability not used) = could have called
_ The opposite of "must have" is "___ have". = can't | cannot | couldn't
_ He must be tired now. (past) → He must have ___ tired. = been
_ I'm not sure where she went. She ___ (go) home. = might have gone | may have gone | could have gone
_ The note is in Japanese. Hakan ___ (write) it — he doesn't know any Japanese. = can't have written | couldn't have written

## Reading: Nine days lost in the Sahara (≈280 words)

> In 1994, an Italian runner named Mauro Prosperi took part in a famous 250-kilometre race across the Sahara Desert. On the fourth day, a terrible sandstorm hit. When it finally stopped, after eight hours, the landscape had completely changed, and Prosperi had no idea where he was.
>
> He was missing for nine days before he was found by a nomadic family, hundreds of kilometres away, in Algeria. Rescuers later tried to understand how he could have survived. He **must have used** his race training, they said, because he walked mainly in the early morning and the evening, and rested in the shade during the hottest hours.
>
> Prosperi himself explained some of his decisions. He drank very little water at a time and made his supply last as long as possible. At one point he found a small building, a shrine, where he slept and caught small animals to eat. A plane flew over him but did not see him. The pilot **can't have noticed** his signal fire, because of the strong light and the distance.
>
> Doctors were surprised that he lost around 16 kilograms but had no permanent damage. Some believe his excellent physical fitness **might have protected** his body. Others think simple luck **may have played** a big part: if the nomads had not passed that way, the story **could have ended** very differently.
>
> Prosperi did not give up running. In fact, he returned to the same desert race several times in later years. When journalists asked why, he said that the desert had taught him not to panic.

? How long was Prosperi missing according to the text?
- forty days ~~ not stated
* nine days ~~ paragraph 2
- four days ~~ the storm happened on day four
- eight hours ~~ that's how long the storm lasted
! Scan for numbers — but check which event each number belongs to.

? "He **must have used** his race training." The writer is:
- not sure at all ~~ *must have* is a strong guess
* almost sure, based on evidence (how he walked and rested) ~~ correct
- sure that it didn't happen ~~ that's *can't have*
- talking about the future ~~ it's about the past
! *must have* = strong deduction about the past.

? Why didn't the pilot help him?
- He didn't want to ~~ not stated
* He probably didn't see the signal fire because of the light and distance ~~ "can't have noticed"
- The plane had no fuel ~~ not stated
- Prosperi hid from the plane ~~ he lit a signal fire
! Detail + modal meaning.

? Which sentence expresses the **weakest** certainty?
- He must have used his training. ~~ strong
- The pilot can't have noticed the fire. ~~ strong (negative)
* Luck may have played a big part. ~~ *may have* = possible
- He lost 16 kilograms. ~~ a fact, not a guess
! *may/might/could have* = possibility.

## Try it

Read the situation and write **five sentences** with past modals.

> *You come home. The front door is open. There is mud on the floor, an empty food bowl, a broken vase, and your dog is asleep on the sofa. Your brother's bag is not there.*

:::reveal Show model answer
1. Somebody **must have left** the door open — probably my brother when he went out.
2. The dog **must have run** outside, because there is mud on the floor.
3. It **might have knocked** over the vase while it was running around.
4. A burglar **can't have come** in — nothing is missing and the dog is calm.
5. My brother **might have fed** the dog before he left, or the dog **could have eaten** everything very quickly!
:::

:::rubric Deduction self-check
- Uses *must have*, *might/may/could have* and *can't have* at least once each
- Always **modal + have + V3** (not *must have went*)
- Each guess is linked to a piece of evidence
- *can't have* (not *mustn't have*) for strong negative guesses
:::

## Common mistakes

- **"He must have went"** → *must have **gone*** (use V3, not V2).
- **"She mustn't have seen it"** for a strong negative guess → *She **can't have** seen it.*
- Writing **"must of"** → *must **have***.
- Using a present modal for a past situation: *He must be at home yesterday* → *He must **have been** at home yesterday*.

## Key terms

- deduction | a conclusion you reach from evidence | çıkarım
- evidence | facts or signs that show something is true | kanıt
- past modal | modal + have + V3, for guesses about the past | geçmiş zaman kip yapısı (must have / might have)
- survive | to stay alive in a dangerous situation | hayatta kalmak
- speculate | to guess without being certain | tahmin yürütmek, spekülasyon yapmak

## Quick check

? "His car isn't outside. He ___ to work already."
- can't have gone ~~ the evidence suggests he did go
* must have gone ~~ strong guess from evidence
- must have went ~~ wrong participle
- mustn't have gone ~~ wrong form for deduction
! Evidence → *must have + V3*.

? Which sentence means "I'm almost sure it didn't happen"?
- She might have forgotten. ~~ only possible
- She must have forgotten. ~~ sure it *did* happen
* She can't have forgotten. ~~ strong negative deduction
- She could have forgotten. ~~ only possible
! *can't have* = strong negative.

? "You ___ me you were coming! I would have cooked." (criticism)
- must have told ~~ a deduction, not criticism
* could have told ~~ an ability you didn't use
- can't have told ~~ wrong meaning
- might told ~~ wrong form
! *could have + V3* can criticise a missed chance.
