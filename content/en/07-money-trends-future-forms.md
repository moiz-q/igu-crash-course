How will we pay for things in ten years? Will cash disappear? Talking about **trends** means talking about the **future** and giving **opinions**. This lesson covers both.

## Five ways to talk about the future

| Form | Structure | Use it for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **will** | will + verb | predictions you *believe*; quick decisions; promises | *I think cash **will disappear**.* · *I'**ll pay**, don't worry.* |
| **going to** | am/is/are going to + verb | plans already decided; predictions from **evidence you can see now** | *I'**m going to open** a savings account.* · *Look at the queue — we'**re going to wait** a long time.* |
| **present continuous** | am/is/are + verb-ing | fixed arrangements with a time/place (in the diary) | *I'**m meeting** the bank adviser on Friday at 3.* |
| **future continuous** | will be + verb-ing | an action **in progress** at a future moment | *This time next year I'**ll be working** part-time.* |
| **future perfect** | will have + past participle | an action **finished before** a future moment | *By 2030, most shops **will have stopped** taking cash.* |

Time markers help: *by* (+ future perfect), *this time next…* / *at 8 p.m. tomorrow* (+ future continuous), *on Friday at 3* (+ present continuous for arrangements).

## Giving and responding to opinions

| Giving | Responding |
| --- | --- |
| In my opinion, … | I see your point, but… |
| I strongly believe that… | That's a good point. |
| As far as I'm concerned, … | I'm not convinced. |
| It seems to me that… | Do you really think so? |
| I'd say that… | I'd go along with that. |
| If you ask me, … | I'm in two minds about it. *(= not sure)* |

## Worked example

> **Mert:** If you ask me, in ten years nobody **will be carrying** cash. We **'ll** all **be paying** with phones.
> **Elif:** I'm not convinced. My grandparents **are going to keep** using cash — they don't trust apps.
> **Mert:** That's a good point. But by then, banks **will have closed** most of their branches.
> **Elif:** Maybe. Anyway, I'**m opening** a digital savings account on Monday — the appointment is at ten.

Notice each choice: *will be carrying* (in progress at a future time), *are going to keep* (intention), *will have closed* (finished before a future time), *I'm opening … on Monday* (fixed arrangement).

## Practice: choose the future form (18 items)

Type the verb in the correct form (use contractions or full forms — both are accepted where shown).

_ Look at those dark clouds. It ___ (rain). = is going to rain | 's going to rain
_ I ___ (meet) my tutor at 2 p.m. tomorrow. It's in my calendar. = am meeting | 'm meeting
_ "The phone is ringing." "I ___ (answer) it!" = will answer | 'll answer
_ This time next week, I ___ (sit) my maths exam. = will be sitting | 'll be sitting
_ By the end of the year, I ___ (save) 5,000 lira. = will have saved | 'll have saved
_ I think online shopping ___ (grow) even more. = will grow | 'll grow
_ We've decided. We ___ (buy) a second-hand laptop. = are going to buy | 're going to buy
_ By 2035, many banks ___ (close) their branches. = will have closed | 'll have closed
_ At 9 p.m. tonight I ___ (watch) the match, so don't call. = will be watching | 'll be watching
_ My sister ___ (fly) to Izmir on Saturday; she has the ticket. = is flying | 's flying
_ Don't worry, I promise I ___ (pay) you back next week. = will pay | 'll pay
_ He hasn't studied at all. He ___ (fail) the test. = is going to fail | 's going to fail
_ By the time you arrive, we ___ (finish) dinner. = will have finished | 'll have finished
_ In 2040, people ___ probably ___ (use) cash at all. = won't ; use
_ I ___ (start) a part-time job next month — I signed the contract yesterday. = am starting | 'm starting | am going to start
_ In my ___, prices will keep rising. = opinion
_ As far as I'm ___, saving is more important than spending. = concerned
_ I'm in two ___ about buying a new phone. = minds

## Reading: Where is our money going? (≈290 words)

> Ten years ago, most people in Türkiye paid for their bread with coins. Today, many young people do not carry a wallet at all. A phone, a watch or a card is enough. This is one of the biggest money trends of our time: the move to **contactless and mobile payment**.
>
> Experts expect this trend to continue. Many believe that by the end of this decade, small shops **will have replaced** their old card machines with QR codes and phone payments. Some economists go further and predict that a few countries **will be using** digital versions of their national currency within ten years.
>
> A second trend is **"buy now, pay later"**. Online shops let customers split a payment into three or four parts without interest. Supporters say this helps students buy useful things, such as a laptop for their studies. Critics are not convinced. In their opinion, it makes spending feel too easy, and some young people **are going to find** themselves with several small debts that add up to a large one.
>
> A third trend is more positive: interest in **saving and investing** is growing among people in their twenties. Free apps show where your money goes each month, and short videos explain basic ideas like budgets and emergency funds.
>
> So what should a student do? Most financial advisers give similar advice. First, make a monthly budget and check it every week. Second, be careful with instalments: add up the total you will pay. Third, try to save a small amount regularly, even if it is only a few hundred lira. As one adviser put it, "Your future self **will be thanking** you."

? What is the **gist** of the text?
- Cash is illegal in Türkiye ~~ not stated
* Three current money trends and some advice for students ~~ covers the whole text
- How to open a bank account ~~ not discussed
- The history of coins ~~ only mentioned briefly
! Look at the topic of each paragraph.

? What do critics think about "buy now, pay later"?
- It is the best way to save ~~ opposite
* It makes spending too easy and can create several small debts ~~ paragraph 3
- It is only for old people ~~ not stated
- It has very high interest ~~ the text says "without interest"
! Critics' opinion is in paragraph 3.

? "By the end of this decade, small shops **will have replaced** their old card machines." The future perfect shows that:
- the replacing will be in progress at the end of the decade ~~ that would be future continuous
* the replacing will be finished before the end of the decade ~~ correct use of future perfect
- it is a sudden decision ~~ that's *will* + verb
- it is an arrangement with a time ~~ that's present continuous
! Future perfect = completed before a future point.

- Make a monthly budget ~~ it is in the text
- Add up the total of instalments ~~ it is in the text
* Never use a bank card ~~ not stated

## Try it

Write **100–120 words**: *"Will cash disappear in Türkiye in the next 15 years?"* Use at least **three different future forms** and **two opinion phrases**.

:::reveal Show model answer
In my opinion, cash will not disappear completely, but it will become much less common. By 2040, most young people will have stopped carrying coins, because phones are faster and safer. This time next decade, we will probably be paying for buses and coffee with our watches. However, I'm not convinced that everyone will change. Many older people and small village shops are going to keep using cash, because they trust what they can hold. As far as I'm concerned, the government should make sure that people who don't use smartphones can still pay easily. So cash is going to become rare, but not extinct.
:::

- Clear opinion in the first sentence
- At least three future forms (e.g. *will*, *going to*, *will be -ing*, *will have + V3*)
- At least two opinion phrases (*In my opinion*, *As far as I'm concerned*…)
- A reason or example for each idea; 100–120 words
:::

## Common mistakes

- **"I will meet my friend tomorrow at 5"** for a fixed plan is not wrong, but native speakers usually say *I'm meeting*.
- **"By 2030 I will finish university"** → *I **will have finished*** (with *by* + future time).
- Using *will* after *when/if* for the future: *When I **will** get a job…* → *When I **get** a job…*
- *"In my opinion, I think…"* repeats the same idea. Use one of them.

## Key terms

- trend | a general direction in which something is changing | eğilim, trend
- future perfect | will have + past participle: finished before a future time | gelecek zamanda bitmiş eylem (future perfect)
- future continuous | will be + -ing: in progress at a future time | gelecekte devam eden eylem
- budget | a plan of how much money you will spend | bütçe
- instalment | one of several payments you make to pay for something | taksit

## Quick check

? "At 10 a.m. tomorrow I ___ in the library, so text me instead of calling."
- will study ~~ simple *will* doesn't show "in progress at 10 a.m."
* will be studying ~~ action in progress at a future moment
- will have studied ~~ that means finished before 10 a.m.
- study ~~ present simple doesn't fit here
! Future continuous for an action in progress at a future time.

? "I've already bought the tickets. We ___ to Antalya in July."
- will go ~~ a decision made before speaking takes *going to* or present continuous
* are going ~~ a fixed arrangement (tickets bought)
- will have gone ~~ not "finished before"
- go ~~ incomplete
! Arrangements → present continuous (or *going to*).

? Which is an **opinion** phrase?
- by the time ~~ a time expression
* as far as I'm concerned ~~ introduces your view
- this time next year ~~ a time expression
- in two weeks ~~ a time expression
! Opinion phrases introduce what *you* think.

? "When I ___ my degree, I'm going to look for a job abroad."
- will finish ~~ no *will* after *when* for the future
* finish ~~ present simple after *when* with a future meaning
- will have finished ~~ no *will* after *when*
- am going to finish ~~ not used after *when* here
! Time clauses (*when, after, as soon as*) use the present for the future.
