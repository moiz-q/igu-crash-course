## Section 1: Grammar (40 pts — 4 points each)

1. Complete with the correct future form: *"Don't call at 8 p.m. — I ___ (watch) the match."*
2. Complete: *"By the end of this year, I ___ (save) enough money for a laptop."*
3. Make one formal sentence with a **preposition + which/whom**: *"This is the lab. We work in the lab every Tuesday."*
4. Rewrite in the **passive**: *"Volunteers have cleaned the beach."*
5. Rewrite in the **passive**: *"They are building a new library."*
6. Correct the mistake: *"I bought a ten-minutes video course from a shoes shop's website."*
7. Write the possessive: *the opinions of the people* → ___
8. Complete with a **past modal**: *"His car is still outside. He ___ (not / leave) yet."* (you are almost sure)
9. Complete with a substitute word: *"Is the library open on Sunday?" "I don't think ___."*
10. Complete: *"Buses are cheap, ___ taxis are expensive."* (contrast)

:::reveal Show Section 1 answers
1. `will be watching` (future continuous — in progress at 8 p.m.)
2. `will have saved` (future perfect with *by*)
3. *This is the lab **in which** we work every Tuesday.* (or *…the lab where we work…*)
4. *The beach **has been cleaned** (by volunteers).*
5. *A new library **is being built**.*
6. *I bought a **ten-minute** video course from a **shoe shop's** website.*
7. *the people's opinions*
8. `can't have left` (strong negative deduction)
9. `so`
10. `whereas` / `while`
:::

## Section 2: Conversation skills (15 pts)

Complete the dialogue with suitable phrases (agreeing/disagreeing, discourse markers).

> **A:** I think the university should close the library at 6 p.m. to save energy.
> **B:** (1) ___ — saving energy is important, but many students study in the evenings.
> **A:** (2) ___, a lot of people only use it to charge their phones. (3) ___, did you hear that the cafeteria is closing too?
> **B:** Really? (4) ___ the cafeteria, the food there is terrible anyway! (5) ___, back to the library — maybe it could stay open but use fewer lights.

:::reveal Show Section 2 model answers
1. *I agree up to a point* / *That's true, but…* (partial agreement)
2. *Actually* / *To be honest* (adding surprising information)
3. *By the way* (new side topic)
4. *Speaking of* (linking to a word just mentioned)
5. *Anyway* (returning to the main topic)
:::

## Section 3: Reading (20 pts)

> Every few years, a new technology promises to "change education forever". In the 1920s it was radio; in the 1960s, television; in the 2010s, free online courses. Each time, experts predicted that teachers would soon be unnecessary. Each time, they were wrong — at least partly.
>
> It is easy to see why the predictions failed. All of these technologies were good at **delivering** information, but learning is not only receiving information. Students need to practise, make mistakes, get feedback and stay motivated. A recorded lecture cannot notice that you look confused. A teacher can.
>
> However, it would be a mistake to say that the technologies changed nothing. Radio brought lessons to farms that had no school nearby. Online courses have allowed millions of people to learn skills that were not taught in their country. The real change was not that teachers disappeared, but that education became available in more places.
>
> Today, artificial intelligence is the newest promise. Some people believe it will replace teachers; others fear it will help students cheat. History suggests a third answer is more likely: AI will become one more tool, useful in the hands of a good teacher and a motivated student, and much less useful without them.

1. What is the **main idea** of the text? (gist)
2. According to paragraph 2, why did the predictions fail? Give **two** things students need.
3. What positive change did radio bring?
4. Is this statement True, False or Not given? *"Online courses are more popular than university courses."*
5. Find a **passive** verb in paragraph 3.

:::reveal Show Section 3 answers
1. New technologies have repeatedly been expected to replace teachers; they didn't, but they did make education available to more people — and AI will probably be another tool, not a replacement.
2. Learning needs more than information: students need practice, mistakes, feedback and motivation (any two).
3. It brought lessons to farms with no nearby school.
4. **Not given** — the text does not compare their popularity.
5. *were not taught* (past simple passive).
:::

## Section 4: Writing (25 pts)

Choose **one** (150–180 words):

**A.** A **comparison paragraph**: *"Learning in a classroom vs. learning online."* (point-by-point, one similarity and two differences)

**B.** A **film or series review** using at least two relative clauses with prepositions.

**C.** An **advantages-and-disadvantages paragraph**: *"Using AI tools for homework."*

:::reveal Show a model answer (A)
Learning in a classroom and learning online share the same goal, but they are different in flexibility and in contact with teachers. Both methods can use the same materials: slides, readings and practice exercises are often identical. However, online learning is far more flexible than classroom learning. Students can watch a lecture at midnight or pause it to take notes, whereas classroom lessons happen at a fixed time and speed. On the other hand, the classroom offers much better contact with teachers and classmates. Unlike an online video, a teacher can see that a student is confused and explain the idea again in a different way. Group discussions and lab work are also easier when everyone is in the same room. In short, online learning is more convenient, but classroom learning is usually more effective for difficult subjects, so a mix of the two is often the best choice.
:::
