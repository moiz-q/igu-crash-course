# English I MCQ Bank (Lessons 02–14, 5+ per lesson)

? [02] While I ___ home yesterday, it started to rain heavily.
- walked ~~ ongoing background action takes Past Continuous
* was walking ~~ Past Continuous interrupted by `started`
- had walked ~~ walking was still in progress, not finished earlier
- am walking ~~ `yesterday` requires past tense
! Use `was walking` for an ongoing past background action.

? [02] By the time we arrived at the station, the train ___.
- left ~~ use Past Perfect to show it happened before we arrived
* had already left ~~ `had + V3` marks the earlier past event
- was leaving ~~ `by the time` with completed departure uses Past Perfect
- has left ~~ cannot mix Present Perfect with `arrived`
! Past Perfect (`had left`) marks an action completed before another past event.

? [02] What ___ at 10 p.m. last night when I called you?
- did you do ~~ asks about a finished action, not what was in progress at 10 p.m.
* were you doing ~~ action in progress at a specific past time
- had you done ~~ asks before 10 p.m.
- are you doing ~~ wrong tense for `last night`
! Specific past moment in progress takes Past Continuous (`were you doing`).

? [02] She couldn't enter the lab because she ___ her student ID card at home.
- leaves ~~ present tense
- was leaving ~~ leaving is not a continuous background here
* had left ~~ happened earlier before arriving at the lab
- has left ~~ `couldn't` is past, so use Past Perfect
! Earlier past cause takes Past Perfect (`had left`).

? [02] Yesterday, Emre ___ his C file, compiled it, and fixed two warnings.
* opened ~~ sequence of completed main events in Past Simple
- was opening ~~ sequential completed actions use Past Simple
- had opened ~~ same timeline sequence
- opens ~~ `yesterday` needs past
! Sequential completed actions in a story use Past Simple.

? [05] That lemon cake smells ___!
- like delicious ~~ `delicious` is an adjective, so omit `like`
* delicious ~~ `sense verb + adjective`
- deliciously ~~ sense verbs take adjectives, not adverbs
- as delicious ~~ `as` is wrong here
! Use `smell + adjective` (`smells delicious`).

? [05] Your laptop fan sounds ___ a tiny helicopter!
- similar ~~ `sounds similar to` or `sounds like`
* like ~~ `sound like + noun`
- as ~~ use `like` before a noun here
- happily ~~ adverb
! Use `sense verb + like + noun` (`sounds like a helicopter`).

? [05] Istanbul Gelisim is ___ university in Istanbul, and my brother waited ___ hour for the shuttle.
- an / a ~~ `university` starts with `/j/` consonant; `hour` starts with vowel sound
* a / an ~~ `a university` (`/juː/`), `an hour` (silent `h`)
- a / a ~~ `hour` starts with vowel sound → `an`
- an / an ~~ `university` starts with consonant sound → `a`
! Choose `a` vs. `an` by **sound**, not spelling.

? [05] ___ computers have changed how ___ students study.
- The / the ~~ general plural nouns take zero article
* Ø / Ø ~~ general plural nouns (`Computers... students`) take no article
- A / a ~~ plural nouns cannot take `a`
- The / a ~~ plural `students` cannot take `a`
! General plural nouns take zero article (`Ø`).

? [05] I bought a wireless mouse yesterday, and ___ mouse already needs a battery.
- a ~~ second mention of a specific item takes `the`
* the ~~ second mention (`the mouse`)
- an ~~ consonant sound and specific
- Ø ~~ singular countable noun needs an article
! Second mention of a known noun takes `the`.

? [06] You ___ bring your student ID to the final exam; security checks everyone at the door.
- don't have to ~~ contradicts mandatory check
* must ~~ strict obligation
- mustn't ~~ means forbidden
- shouldn't ~~ illogical
! Mandatory rules use `must` or `have to`.

? [06] You ___ pay for Cisco Packet Tracer—it is free for students.
- mustn't ~~ `mustn't` means using it for free is forbidden
* don't have to ~~ no obligation / unnecessary
- must ~~ contradicts "free"
- had to ~~ past obligation
! Lack of necessity uses `don't have to`.

? [06] Students ___ share their portal passwords with anyone.
- don't have to ~~ implies it's optional
* mustn't ~~ strictly prohibited
- have to ~~ illogical
- might ~~ weak possibility
! Use `mustn't` for strict prohibition.

? [06] Yesterday evening, we ___ restart the router three times.
- must ~~ `must` has no past tense
* had to ~~ past obligation uses `had to`
- have to ~~ present tense
- musted ~~ invalid word
! Past obligation is always `had to`.

? [06] Which sentence is the clearest **instruction** in a user manual?
- Clicking Save before closing. ~~ `-ing` is not an imperative
* First, click Save before closing the window. ~~ sequence linker + base imperative
- You clicking Save now. ~~ missing verb
- To click Save please. ~~ unnatural
! Manuals use sequence linkers + base imperative verbs.

? [02] By 1900, she ___ in the same hospital for thirty years.
- worked ~~ past simple doesn't show duration up to a past moment
- was working ~~ past continuous doesn't link to an earlier time
* had been working ~~ past perfect continuous: duration up to 1900
- has been working ~~ present perfect can't be used with *By 1900*
! *had been + V-ing* shows a long action up to a past moment.

? [03] Which phrase shows complete agreement?
- I'm not so sure. ~~ doubt / disagreement
* I couldn't agree more. ~~ means "I agree 100%"
- I see what you mean, but… ~~ partial agreement
- To some extent. ~~ partial agreement
! *I couldn't agree more* = full agreement.

? [03] "Homework is a waste of time." Which reply is the most polite disagreement?
- That's nonsense. ~~ rude
- You're wrong. ~~ too direct
* I take your point, but I think it helps us practise. ~~ acknowledges, then disagrees with a reason
- No way! ~~ informal and blunt
! Acknowledge first, then disagree with a reason.

? [03] Choose the correct sentence.
- I am agree with your idea. ~~ never *am agree*
* I agree with your idea. ~~ correct
- I agreeing your idea. ~~ wrong form, missing *with*
- I am agreed you. ~~ wrong
! *agree* is a normal verb.

? [03] "I agree **up to a point**" means:
- I agree completely ~~ no
* I agree with part of it ~~ partial agreement
- I don't understand ~~ no
- I want to change the topic ~~ no
! *Up to a point* = partly.

? [03] Which question asks for someone's opinion?
* What's your view on this? ~~ asks for opinion
- Where is the library? ~~ asks for information
- Can I borrow your pen? ~~ a request
- When does the class start? ~~ asks for a time
! *What's your view / What do you think / How do you feel about…?*

? [04] Reading quickly to understand the main idea of a text is called:
* skimming ~~ reading for gist
- scanning ~~ looking for one specific detail
- translating ~~ no
- dictating ~~ no
! Skimming = gist; scanning = detail.

? [04] A statement says "The museum opened in 1950." The text never mentions when the museum opened. The answer is:
- True ~~ not supported
- False ~~ the text doesn't contradict it
* Not given ~~ the information is not in the text
- Partly true ~~ not an option in this task type
! Not given = the text says nothing about it.

? [04] An animal that is in danger of disappearing completely is:
- domestic ~~ lives with people
* endangered ~~ correct
- stray ~~ has no home
- wild ~~ lives in nature, not necessarily in danger
! *Endangered species* = nesli tükenmekte olan tür.

? [04] Before you listen to a recording in an exam, you should first:
* read the questions and underline key words ~~ you know what to listen for
- write the answers ~~ you haven't heard it yet
- close your eyes ~~ no
- translate every question into Turkish ~~ wastes time
! Know what you are listening for.

? [04] The question uses the word "pets"; the text says "companion animals". This shows that exam questions often use:
- the same words as the text ~~ often they don't
* synonyms ~~ different words with the same meaning
- grammar mistakes ~~ no
- Turkish words ~~ no
! Look for synonyms, not only identical words.

? [07] "This time tomorrow I ___ on the plane to London."
- will sit ~~ doesn't show "in progress" at that time
* will be sitting ~~ future continuous
- will have sat ~~ means finished
- sit ~~ wrong
! *This time tomorrow* + future continuous.

? [07] "By 2030, I ___ university."
- will finish ~~ *by* + future time needs future perfect
* will have finished ~~ finished before 2030
- am finishing ~~ an arrangement, not "finished by"
- finish ~~ wrong
! *by + time* → future perfect.

? [07] "Look at that car! It ___ into the wall!" (you can see it happening)
- will crash ~~ prediction without evidence
* is going to crash ~~ prediction based on present evidence
- will have crashed ~~ wrong
- crashes ~~ wrong
! Evidence now → *going to*.

? [07] "I can't come on Friday. I ___ my cousin at the airport at 6." (it's arranged)
- will meet ~~ less natural for an arrangement
* am meeting ~~ present continuous for a fixed arrangement
- will have met ~~ wrong meaning
- meet ~~ wrong
! Diary arrangements → present continuous.

? [07] Which phrase introduces **your own** opinion?
- I'm not convinced. ~~ a response to someone else
* As far as I'm concerned, … ~~ gives your view
- Do you really think so? ~~ a question
- I'd go along with that. ~~ agreeing with someone else
! *In my opinion / As far as I'm concerned / If you ask me…*

? [08] Choose the correct formal sentence.
- The man with who she works is Italian. ~~ after a preposition use *whom*
* The man with whom she works is Italian. ~~ correct
- The man with that she works is Italian. ~~ no *that* after a preposition
- The man whom she works is Italian. ~~ preposition missing
! Preposition + *whom* (people).

? [08] "The village ___ the film was shot is near Antalya."
- in that ~~ no *that* after a preposition
* in which ~~ correct (= where)
- which ~~ preposition missing (or put *in* at the end)
- in whom ~~ *whom* is for people
! *in which* = *where* for places.

? [08] "The film has ten characters, most of ___ are teenagers."
- who ~~ after *of* use *whom*
* whom ~~ *most of whom*
- them ~~ creates two sentences without a link
- which ~~ for things
! Quantity + *of whom / of which*.

? [08] What usually comes at the **end** of a film review?
- a list of all the actors' birthdays ~~ no
* a recommendation (who should watch it) or a star rating ~~ typical ending
- the full ending of the story ~~ that would be a spoiler
- the price of the cinema ticket ~~ no
! Reviews end with an overall opinion and recommendation.

? [08] "The song ___ which the film ends is beautiful." Choose the preposition.
* with ~~ the film ends **with** a song
- on ~~ wrong collocation
- at ~~ wrong
- for ~~ wrong
! *end with* → *the song with which the film ends*.

? [09] Choose the passive: "Workers built the bridge in 1973."
- The bridge built in 1973. ~~ *be* is missing
* The bridge was built in 1973. ~~ past simple passive
- The bridge has built in 1973. ~~ not passive
- The bridge was build in 1973. ~~ wrong participle
! Past passive: *was/were + V3*.

? [09] "Plastic bags ___ in many countries since 2015."
- were banned ~~ *since* needs present perfect
* have been banned ~~ present perfect passive
- are banned since ~~ wrong tense with *since*
- banned ~~ not passive
! *since* → present perfect (passive: *have been + V3*).

? [09] "A new recycling system ___ at the moment."
- is tested ~~ doesn't show "now"
* is being tested ~~ present continuous passive
- has tested ~~ not passive
- tests ~~ not passive
! *at the moment* → *is being + V3*.

? [09] Which phrase starts the **conclusion** of a talk?
- First of all ~~ beginning
- Let's move on to ~~ middle
* To sum up ~~ conclusion
- Today I'd like to talk about ~~ introduction
! *To sum up / In conclusion*.

? [09] Which sentence **cannot** be made passive?
- They recycle paper. ~~ can: *Paper is recycled*
- Someone stole my bike. ~~ can: *My bike was stolen*
* The accident happened at night. ~~ *happen* has no object
- People speak English here. ~~ can: *English is spoken here*
! Only verbs with an object have a passive.

? [10] Choose the correct compound noun.
- a ten-minutes walk ~~ the first part stays singular
* a ten-minute walk ~~ correct
- a ten-minute's walk ~~ no apostrophe
- a walk ten minutes ~~ wrong order
! Number + singular noun before another noun.

? [10] "This is my ___ laptop." (it belongs to my brother)
* brother's ~~ possessive 's
- brothers ~~ plural, not possessive
- brothers' ~~ means several brothers
- brother ~~ needs 's for possession
! Singular owner → *'s*.

? [10] Which is correct for things?
- the car's door ~~ we usually say *the car door*
* the car door ~~ compound noun for parts of things
- the door of car ~~ article missing
- the door's car ~~ wrong meaning
! For things, use a compound noun.

? [10] Which phrase introduces a **disadvantage**?
- One advantage is that… ~~ advantage
* A major drawback is that… ~~ disadvantage
- Another benefit is… ~~ advantage
- Overall, … ~~ conclusion
! *drawback / disadvantage / On the other hand*.

? [10] "The ___ books are on the second floor." (books for children)
- childrens ~~ apostrophe needed
- childrens' ~~ *children* is already plural
* children's ~~ irregular plural + 's
- child's ~~ one child only
! *children's, women's, people's*.

? [11] "Did you take the bus?" "___, I walked. It's only ten minutes."
* Actually ~~ corrects the guess
- By the way ~~ changes topic
- Mind you ~~ adds a limiting point
- Speaking of ~~ needs a link word
! *Actually* = in fact (correction).

? [11] Which marker **returns** to your point after an interruption?
- By the way ~~ new side topic
* As I was saying ~~ correct
- To be honest ~~ gives a true opinion
- Speaking of ~~ links a new topic
! *As I was saying*.

? [11] "The hotel was great. ___, the breakfast was disappointing."
- Speaking of ~~ needs a linked noun
- By the way ~~ not a new topic
* Mind you ~~ adds a point that limits "great"
- As I was saying ~~ no interruption
! *Mind you* = but remember…

? [11] "Actually" in English means:
- currently / now ~~ a common mistake (Turkish *aktüel*)
* in fact ~~ correct
- finally ~~ no
- immediately ~~ no
! *Actually* = aslında.

? [11] "We missed the ferry, lost our bags, and it rained. ___, we had a great time in the end."
* Anyway ~~ returns to the main point
- Actually ~~ not a correction
- Mind you ~~ wrong direction
- By the way ~~ not a side topic
! *Anyway* moves past details to the main point.

? [12] "The streets are wet. It ___ in the night."
* must have rained ~~ strong deduction from evidence
- can't have rained ~~ opposite of the evidence
- must rain ~~ present, not past
- must have rain ~~ needs V3 *rained*
! *must have + V3*.

? [12] "She ___ the message — her phone was switched off all day."
- must have read ~~ the evidence says she couldn't
* can't have read ~~ strong negative deduction
- mustn't have read ~~ not used for deduction
- might read ~~ not past
! Opposite of *must have* = *can't have*.

? [12] "I'm not sure why he's late. He ___ in traffic."
- must have got stuck ~~ too certain
* might have got stuck ~~ possible
- can't have got stuck ~~ too certain (negative)
- might got stuck ~~ needs *have*
! *might/may/could have* = possibility.

? [12] Which sentence is grammatically correct?
- He must have went home. ~~ needs V3 *gone*
- He must of gone home. ~~ write *have*, not *of*
* He must have gone home. ~~ correct
- He must had gone home. ~~ wrong
! modal + **have** + V3.

? [12] "You ___ me! I waited for an hour." (you didn't tell me; criticism)
- must have told ~~ deduction, not criticism
* could have told ~~ a possible action that didn't happen
- can't have told ~~ wrong meaning
- might tell ~~ not past
! *could have + V3* = you had the chance but didn't.

? [13] "My phone is broken. I need a new ___."
* one ~~ replaces "phone"
- it ~~ *it* = the same broken phone
- ones ~~ plural
- so ~~ replaces clauses
! *one* = another of the same kind.

? [13] "Will the exam be difficult?" "I hope ___."
- no ~~ wrong form
* not ~~ *I hope not*
- so not ~~ wrong
- don't ~~ wrong
! *hope so / hope not*.

? [13] "She said she would call, and she ___."
* did ~~ replaces "called"
- was ~~ wrong auxiliary
- so ~~ wrong
- one ~~ wrong
! *do/does/did* replaces a verb phrase.

? [13] A story about a frightening future society where people are controlled is a:
- utopia ~~ a perfect society
* dystopia ~~ correct
- biography ~~ a life story
- comedy ~~ no
! *dystopia* ≠ *utopia*.

? [13] "I prefer the red shoes to the black ___."
- one ~~ plural noun needs *ones*
* ones ~~ correct
- it ~~ wrong
- them ones ~~ wrong
! *ones* for plural countable nouns.

? [14] "Trains are fast, ___ buses are cheap."
* whereas ~~ shows contrast between two clauses
- similarly ~~ shows similarity
- both ~~ wrong position
- like ~~ needs a noun
! *whereas / while* + clause.

? [14] Choose the correct comparative.
- more cheaper ~~ double comparative
* much cheaper ~~ intensifier + comparative
- most cheaper ~~ wrong
- cheaper more ~~ wrong
! *much / far / a lot* + comparative.

? [14] Which linking word shows a **similarity**?
- However ~~ difference
- In contrast ~~ difference
* Likewise ~~ similarity
- Unlike ~~ difference
! *Similarly / Likewise / Like / Both*.

? [14] In the point-by-point method you:
- describe A completely, then B completely ~~ block method
* compare A and B on each point, one point at a time ~~ correct
- write only about one item ~~ no comparison
- write a list of words ~~ no
! Point-by-point = both items for each point.

? [14] "Real robots are not ___ intelligent ___ film robots."
* as … as ~~ *not as … as* = less
- so … that ~~ different structure
- more … as ~~ wrong
- as … than ~~ wrong
! *not as + adjective + as*.
