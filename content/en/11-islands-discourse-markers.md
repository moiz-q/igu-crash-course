In real conversations people change topic, correct themselves, go back to an earlier point and add surprising information. Small words called **discourse markers** do this job. They don't change the grammar, but they help the listener follow you.

## Common discourse markers

| Marker | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **by the way** | introduces a new, less important topic | *We loved Büyükada. **By the way**, did you get my photos?* |
| **anyway** | returns to the main topic, or ends a topic | *…the ferry was late, the café was closed… **Anyway**, we finally got there.* |
| **actually** | corrects someone or adds surprising information | *"You stayed a week?" "**Actually**, only three days."* |
| **as I was saying** | returns to what you said before an interruption | *Sorry about that. **As I was saying**, the beaches are quiet in May.* |
| **speaking of…** | links to a new topic using a word just mentioned | *"We ate fresh fish." "**Speaking of** food, have you tried Bozcaada's grapes?"* |
| **mind you** | adds a point that makes the previous one less strong | *It was beautiful. **Mind you**, it was very expensive.* |
| **to be honest** | gives a true, sometimes negative opinion | ***To be honest**, I prefer the mountains.* |
| **I mean** | explains or corrects what you just said | *It was crowded — **I mean**, really packed.* |
| **you know** | checks the listener is following (informal) | *That small island, **you know**, the one with no cars.* |
| **so** | starts a topic or a conclusion | ***So**, where shall we go next summer?* |
| **right / OK then** | closes a topic or a conversation | ***Right**, I have to go. See you!* |

**Careful:** *actually* does **not** mean *şu anda* (now). It means *in fact / aslında*. "Now" is *currently* or *at the moment*.

## Worked example

> **Ece:** Did you have a good time on Gökçeada?
> **Can:** Brilliant! The water was so clear. **Mind you**, the wind was strong every afternoon.
> **Ece:** **Speaking of** wind, did you try windsurfing?
> **Can:** **Actually**, I did! I fell in about twenty times. **Anyway**, it was fun.
> **Ece:** Ha! **By the way**, how did you get there?
> **Can:** By ferry from Kabatepe. **As I was saying**, the best part was the villages — **I mean**, the old stone houses.
> **Ece:** **Right**, I think I'm going next year.

## Reading: a conversation about a trip (≈260 words)

> **Selin:** Welcome back! How was the island?
> **Burak:** Honestly? Amazing. Bozcaada is so small that you can cycle around it in a day. Mind you, the hills are steep, so "a day" means a very tired day.
> **Selin:** Ha! Did you stay in a hotel?
> **Burak:** Actually, no. We stayed with a family who rent rooms in their house. They made breakfast every morning — fresh tomatoes, cheese, local jam…
> **Selin:** Speaking of food, is it true that the island is famous for wine and grapes?
> **Burak:** Yes, grapes everywhere. Anyway, the best thing for me was the sunset at the lighthouse on the west side. Everyone goes there in the evening and just sits quietly.
> **Selin:** That sounds lovely. By the way, how much did the ferry cost?
> **Burak:** It's cheap for foot passengers, but if you take a car it's much more. To be honest, you don't need a car at all.
> **Selin:** Good to know. I mean, I don't have a car anyway!
> **Burak:** [phone rings] Sorry, one second… OK. As I was saying, go in June or September. In August it's really crowded — I mean, you can't find a table anywhere.
> **Selin:** Right, June it is. Thanks, Burak!

? Why does Burak say "Mind you, the hills are steep"?
- To change the topic ~~ it stays on the topic of cycling
* To make his positive point about cycling a little less strong ~~ "mind you" adds a limiting point
- To go back to an earlier point ~~ that's "as I was saying"
- To end the conversation ~~ no
! *Mind you* adds a "but" to what was just said.

? "**Actually**, no. We stayed with a family." Here *actually* is used to:
- talk about now ~~ *actually* doesn't mean "now"
* correct Selin's guess that he stayed in a hotel ~~ correcting / surprising information
- agree with Selin ~~ he says "no"
- introduce a new topic ~~ same topic
! *Actually* = in fact (correction).

? Which marker does Burak use after the phone call to return to his topic?
- by the way ~~ introduces a new topic
- speaking of ~~ links to a word just said
* as I was saying ~~ returns to an interrupted point
- right ~~ closes the topic
! *As I was saying* = going back after an interruption.

? When does Burak recommend going?
- August ~~ he says it's crowded then
* June or September ~~ detail after the phone call
- Only in winter ~~ not stated
- Any time ~~ he gives specific months
! Detail question.

:::rubric Conversation self-check
- At least six different discourse markers, each used for the right job
- *Actually* used for correction or surprise (not "now")
- An interruption followed by *As I was saying*
- Sounds natural: short turns, questions and reactions

## Common mistakes

- Using **actually** to mean "now": *Actually I live in Avcılar* (meaning *currently*) → *I **currently** live in Avcılar*.
- **"By the way"** for the main topic. It introduces a *side* topic.
- Overusing *you know* and *I mean* in formal speaking or writing.
- Writing *any way* (two words) for the discourse marker → *anyway*.

## Key terms

- discourse marker | a word or phrase that organises speech (anyway, by the way) | söylem belirleyici
- topic shift | changing the subject of a conversation | konu değiştirme
- interruption | stopping someone while they are speaking | söz kesme
- actually | in fact (used to correct or surprise) | aslında
- ferry | a boat that carries people (and cars) on a regular route | feribot, vapur

## Quick check

? Which marker introduces a **new, less important** topic?
- as I was saying ~~ goes back to an old point
* by the way ~~ introduces a side topic
- anyway ~~ returns to the main topic
- mind you ~~ adds a limiting point
! *By the way* = "this is not the main topic, but…"

? "You're a third-year student, aren't you?" "___, I'm in my first year."
- Anyway ~~ doesn't correct
* Actually ~~ corrects the wrong information
- By the way ~~ new topic
- Speaking of ~~ needs a linked word
! *Actually* corrects.

? "The trip was cheap. ___, the hotel was not very clean."
- By the way ~~ not a new topic
- As I was saying ~~ no interruption
* Mind you ~~ adds a point that limits "cheap"
- Speaking of ~~ no link word
! *Mind you* = but remember that…
