## Part 1: Grammar (15 items)

_ While I ___ (walk) across campus yesterday, I saw my advisor. = was walking ~~ Past Continuous background
_ By the time the lecture ended, the students ___ (take) three pages of notes. = had taken ~~ Past Perfect
_ Could you tell me when ___ (the library / close) on Saturdays? = the library closes ~~ indirect question word order
_ She has been learning C programming ___ October. = since ~~ starting point takes `since`
_ How many SQL challenges ___ (you / solve) today? = have you solved ~~ `How many` = Present Perfect Simple
_ You ___ (mustn't / don't have to) submit a paper copy; the online PDF is enough. = don't have to | do not have to ~~ optional, not forbidden
_ That new monitor looks ___ (sharp) than the old classroom projector. = sharper ~~ comparative
_ He waited for ___ hour outside the IT support office. = an ~~ silent `h` → vowel sound
_ That idea sounds ___ (brilliant)! = brilliant ~~ `sound + adjective`
_ If I ___ (have) more free time this weekend, I would build a small game. = had ~~ Second Conditional
_ If you press `F5` in PowerPoint, the slideshow ___ (start) from Slide 1. = starts ~~ Zero Conditional
_ All student passwords must ___ (encrypt) before saving to the database. = be encrypted ~~ modal passive
_ The researcher ___ paper won the award teaches at İGÜ. = whose ~~ possessive relative pronoun
_ ___ (Although / Despite) the heavy rain, everyone arrived on time. = Despite ~~ `Despite + noun phrase`
_ I forgot my charger; ___, my laptop shut down during the quiz. = therefore | consequently | as a result ~~ result transition

## Part 2: Vocabulary & Collocations (10 items)

_ Please ___ attention to the subnet mask before assigning host IPs. = pay ~~ `pay attention`
_ Always ___ a backup before editing `seed.sql`. = make | take ~~ `make/take a backup`
_ Engineers often study how animals ___ to extreme climates. = adapt ~~ `adapt to`
_ Many old dial-up modems are now completely ___ (out of date). = obsolete ~~ synonym
_ The bug was ___ (very minor)—just one missing comma. = trivial ~~ context vocabulary
_ Scanning a passage means looking quickly for a ___ fact or number. = specific ~~ reading strategy
_ Using `BCC` protects recipients' ___ when emailing a large group. = privacy ~~ email etiquette
_ You must ___ (double-check) your work before submitting. = verify ~~ academic verb
_ Could you ___ me a favor and check my slide contrast? = do ~~ `do someone a favor`
_ Good technical writing uses clear ___ words like *however* and *therefore*. = transition | linking ~~ writing term

## Part 3: Reading Comprehension (2 original passages)

### Passage A: Why Sleep Matters When Learning to Code

> Many first-year university students believe that staying awake until 3:00 a.m. before a coding deadline is a badge of honor. However, neuroscience research consistently shows that sleep is not wasted time—it is the exact moment when the brain organizes new skills. During deep sleep and REM sleep, the brain replays patterns practiced during the day, moving fragile short-term memories into stable long-term storage.
>
> In one well-known experiment, students learned a complex sequence of finger movements on a keyboard. Those who practiced in the morning and were tested twelve hours later improved only slightly. In contrast, students who practiced in the evening, slept eight hours, and were tested the next morning performed twenty percent faster and made far fewer errors—without any extra practice!
>
> The same principle applies to debugging C pointers or memorizing network ports. When you stare at a bug at 1:00 a.m., tired eyes miss obvious typos like `=` instead of `==`. Going to sleep after forty-five minutes of focused study often lets you spot the mistake in ten seconds the next morning. Consistent daily study paired with seven to eight hours of sleep beats last-minute all-nighters every single time.

? According to Passage A, what happens to new skills while you sleep?
- The brain erases short-term memories completely ~~ it moves them to long-term storage
* The brain replays daytime patterns and stores them in long-term memory ~~ improves speed and accuracy
- You need extra midnight practice to improve ~~ the evening group improved without extra practice
- Coding errors increase by twenty percent ~~ errors decreased
! Sleep consolidates newly practiced patterns into long-term memory.

? Why do tired students miss simple bugs like `=` instead of `==` late at night?
- Compilers turn off warnings after midnight ~~ compilers don't care about the clock
* Fatigue reduces focus, whereas resting lets the brain spot mistakes quickly the next morning ~~ 45 min study + sleep beats all-nighters
- Keyboards break at night ~~ unrelated
- Sleep removes C syntax from memory ~~ opposite
! Restored focus after sleep makes spotting simple typos much faster.

### Passage B: The Rise of Micro-Credentials in Tech

> A decade ago, a university diploma was almost the only document employers checked when hiring junior IT staff. Today, while a degree remains essential for deep computer science foundations, companies increasingly look at **micro-credentials**—short, verified certificates in focused skills such as cloud administration, SQL databases, or network security.
>
> Part of this shift comes from speed. University curricula update carefully over years, whereas software tools evolve every few months. By completing a two-week lab badge or an industry networking certificate alongside their degree, students show employers two strengths at once: solid academic theory and up-to-date hands-on practice.
>
> Still, hiring managers warn against collecting dozens of certificates without real understanding. A badge only helps if you can explain the concepts in an interview or demonstrate them in a small project. The smartest approach is combining your degree coursework with two or three targeted practical skills—such as Linux basics, Git, and packet analysis—that directly match the career path you want.

? What is the main advantage of combining a university degree with targeted micro-credentials?
- Micro-credentials replace university degrees completely ~~ degree remains essential
* They pair strong academic foundations with up-to-date practical hands-on skills ~~ shows both theory and practice
- They guarantee a job without an interview ~~ interviews still test real understanding
- They take ten years to earn ~~ short focused certificates
! Combining degree theory with current hands-on skills makes graduates stand out.

? What warning do hiring managers give about certificates?
- Never earn more than one certificate ~~ 2–3 targeted skills are encouraged
* Collecting badges without genuine understanding or project proof does not impress interviewers ~~ must be able to explain and apply them
- Only Linux certificates are legal ~~ Linux/Git/packets are just examples
- Degrees are useless ~~ degrees remain essential
! Certificates must be backed by real understanding and projects.

## Part 4: Listening Practice (Read or have a partner read aloud)

### Listening Transcript 1 — Campus IT Help Desk
> **Staff:** Good morning, İGÜ IT Help Desk, how can I help?
> **Student:** Hi! I'm trying to connect my laptop to the campus Wi-Fi, `IGU-Student`, but it keeps asking for a certificate and won't load any web pages.
> **Staff:** Let's check. First, did you enter your student email address or just your student number as the username?
> **Student:** Just my student number.
> **Staff:** Ah, that's why! You need your full `@ogr.gelisim.edu.tr` email address and your portal password. Also, if your clock date is wrong, the security certificate will fail.
> **Student:** Oh! My laptop battery died yesterday and the clock reset to 2020. Let me fix the date and log in with my full email. Thanks!

? Why did the student's Wi-Fi login fail? (Choose the **best** complete answer)
- The campus Wi-Fi was turned off ~~ other students were connected
* The student used only a student number instead of the full email, AND the laptop's clock had reset to 2020 ~~ both issues blocked login and certificate check
- The laptop had no Wi-Fi card ~~ it saw `IGU-Student`
- The staff member forgot the password ~~ staff diagnosed the two issues
! Both the username format (full email) and wrong system clock (2020) caused the failure.

## Part 5: Writing Prompt (150–180 words)

Write a well-structured paragraph or short two-paragraph essay on **one** of these topics:
1. **Should university programming students learn English alongside coding from Semester 1? Why or why not?**
2. **Describe one technology skill you have learned recently and explain how you plan to use it in the future.**

:::reveal Show model answer (Topic 1)
Learning English alongside programming from Semester 1 is essential for every Computer Programming student. First, almost all programming languages, compiler error messages, and official documentation are written in English. When a student can read error messages and technical guides directly, they solve bugs much faster without waiting for translations. In addition, international software teams communicate in English every day through code reviews, emails, and project tickets.

Although studying both technical subjects and a second language takes consistent effort, small daily habits make it manageable. For example, reading one short technical article a day and writing clear, polite emails in English builds confidence steadily over the semester. Therefore, treating English as a core engineering tool—rather than an extra hurdle—prepares students for both university exams and modern tech careers.
:::

:::rubric B2 Writing self-check
- Clear topic sentence in Paragraph 1
- Two or more concrete supporting reasons/examples
- Smooth transitions (`First`, `In addition`, `Although`, `For example`, `Therefore`)
- Varied grammar (modals, conditionals/passive/present perfect where natural)
- 150–180 words, formal/neutral academic tone
:::
