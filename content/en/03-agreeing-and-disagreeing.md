In seminars and group projects you will often give your view on someone else's idea. English speakers rarely say just "Yes" or "No". They use **softening phrases** so that the conversation stays friendly, even when they disagree.

## Three kinds of response

| You want to… | Useful phrases | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agree** | I completely agree. · That's exactly what I think. · You're absolutely right. · Good point. · I couldn't agree more. | *"Group work is useful." — "I couldn't agree more."* |
| **Partially agree** | I agree up to a point, but… · That's true, but… · I see what you mean, but… · You have a point, however… · To some extent, yes. | *"Online classes are better." — "I agree up to a point, but labs need to be in person."* |
| **Disagree (politely)** | I'm not sure about that. · I'm afraid I don't agree. · I see it differently. · I take your point, but I think… · Actually, I'd say… | *"Exams are useless." — "I'm not sure about that. They make us revise."* |

### Making disagreement softer

- Start with something positive: **"That's an interesting idea, but…"**
- Use *I think / I feel / in my opinion* to show it is your view, not a fact.
- Avoid direct words like *"You're wrong"* or *"That's stupid"*. They sound rude in English.
- **"I couldn't agree more"** means you agree **100%** (not that you disagree). *"I couldn't care less"* means you don't care at all.

### Asking for others' opinions

*What do you think? · Do you agree? · How do you feel about…? · What's your view on…?*

## Worked example

Read this short discussion between three students about phones in class.

> **Deniz:** I think phones should be banned in lectures.
> **Sara:** I agree up to a point. They are distracting, but we sometimes use them to look up words.
> **Ali:** I'm afraid I don't agree with a total ban. We're adults; we can decide ourselves.
> **Deniz:** That's a fair point, but some people text the whole lesson.
> **Sara:** Exactly. Maybe the answer is a rule: phones on silent, only for class work.
> **Ali:** I couldn't agree more. That's a good compromise.

Notice: nobody says "No." Each speaker **reacts** (agree / partly agree / disagree), **gives a reason**, and the group reaches a **compromise**.

## Reading: The four-day week debate

> Last month our university's student council held an open debate. The question was simple: should students have classes on only four days a week? About sixty people came, and the discussion was lively but polite.
>
> The first speaker, a second-year engineering student, was strongly in favour. She argued that a free Friday would give students time for part-time jobs, sport and self-study. "Many of us work in the evenings," she said. "A fifth free day would reduce stress." Several people in the audience nodded.
>
> The second speaker agreed up to a point. He accepted that students are tired, but he worried about the timetable. "If we put five days of lessons into four," he explained, "some days will finish at eight in the evening. That is not less stress; it is just different stress." This comment made many people think again.
>
> A lecturer who was invited as a guest disagreed with the whole idea. She said politely that she understood the wish for more free time, but she believed that daily contact with teachers is important, especially in the first year. "Learning a language or a programming skill needs small steps every day," she said.
>
> In the end, the council did not take a final decision. Instead, it suggested a test: one department would try the four-day week next term, and students would complete a survey at the end. Most people agreed that this was a sensible compromise, because it allowed the university to collect real evidence instead of only opinions.

? What is the **main idea** (gist) of the text?
- Students want longer holidays ~~ holidays are not discussed
* A polite debate about a four-day week ended with a plan to test the idea ~~ this covers the whole text
- A lecturer cancelled Friday classes ~~ no classes were cancelled
- Engineering students work too much ~~ only one detail, not the main idea
! The gist is the general message of the whole text, not a single detail.

? How did the second speaker respond to the first speaker?
- He agreed completely ~~ he had a worry, so not completely
* He partly agreed: students are tired, but long days would cause other stress ~~ "agreed up to a point"
- He refused to speak ~~ he gave a clear argument
- He disagreed with everything ~~ he accepted that students are tired
! "Agreed up to a point" means partial agreement.

? Why did the lecturer disagree?
- She wanted longer days ~~ not mentioned
- She does not like students ~~ she was polite and understanding
* She thinks daily contact and small daily steps help first-year learning ~~ stated in paragraph 4
- She works on Fridays only ~~ not mentioned
! Her reason: skills need "small steps every day".

? What did the council decide?
- To start the four-day week for everyone ~~ no final decision was taken
* To test the idea in one department and survey students ~~ stated in the last paragraph
- To cancel the debate ~~ the debate happened
- To ask the government ~~ not mentioned
! A trial with a survey was the compromise.

## Try it

Write a short dialogue (6–8 lines) between two classmates about this topic: **"All university lectures should be recorded and put online."** One person agrees, the other partly agrees, and they reach a compromise. Use at least **four** different phrases from the table.

:::reveal Show model answer
**A:** I think all lectures should be recorded. Then we can watch them again before exams.
**B:** I agree up to a point. Recordings are useful, but some students might stop coming to class.
**A:** That's a fair point. But people who are ill or working could still follow the course.
**B:** True. I'm just not sure that a video can replace asking the teacher a question.
**A:** I see what you mean. Maybe recordings should be available only after the lesson, as revision.
**B:** I couldn't agree more. That's a good compromise.
:::

:::rubric Dialogue self-check
- At least one clear **agree** phrase and one **partly agree** phrase
- Every opinion has a **reason** (because…, then…, but…)
- No rude language (*You're wrong*, *That's stupid*)
- The speakers reach a compromise at the end
:::

## Common mistakes

- **"I am agree."** → *I agree.* (*agree* is a verb; no *am*.)
- **"I agree with this idea" for a person**: use *agree **with** someone/something*, but *agree **to** do* something (*He agreed to help*).
- Thinking **"I couldn't agree more"** is negative. It means *I agree completely*.
- Saying only *"No"* or *"You are wrong"*. In English this sounds rude; soften it.

## Key terms

- partial agreement | agreeing with some of an idea but not all of it | kısmen katılma
- compromise | a solution where both sides accept part of what they wanted | uzlaşma
- softener | a phrase that makes disagreement more polite | yumuşatıcı ifade
- gist | the general meaning of a text or conversation | ana fikir

## Quick check

? Which response shows **partial** agreement?
- I couldn't agree more. ~~ this is complete agreement
* I agree up to a point, but it's too expensive. ~~ agrees with part, then adds "but"
- I'm afraid I don't agree. ~~ this is disagreement
- Exactly! ~~ complete agreement
! "Up to a point", "to some extent" and "That's true, but…" show partial agreement.

? Which sentence is grammatically correct?
- I am agree with you. ~~ *agree* is a verb; don't add *am*
- I agreeing with you. ~~ missing a correct verb form
* I agree with you. ~~ subject + verb + with + person
- I am agree to you. ~~ two mistakes: *am* and *to*
! Say *I agree* / *I don't agree* — never *I am agree*.

? What is the most **polite** way to disagree?
- You're wrong. ~~ too direct; sounds rude
- That's a stupid idea. ~~ offensive
* I see what you mean, but I see it differently. ~~ acknowledges the other view first
- No. ~~ too short and blunt
! Acknowledge the other person's view, then give yours.

? "I couldn't agree more" means:
- I disagree a little ~~ the opposite
* I agree completely ~~ it is impossible to agree more
- I can't decide ~~ not the meaning
- I don't care ~~ that is "I couldn't care less"
! It is a strong way to agree.
