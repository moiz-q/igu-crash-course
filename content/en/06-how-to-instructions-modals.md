Technical manuals, lab guides, and README files rely on **imperatives**, **sequence linkers**, and **modals of obligation** (`must`, `have to`, `should`).

## Imperatives & sequence linkers

- **Imperative** = base verb (no subject): *Click Save.* / *Do not unplug the cable.*
- **Sequence**: *First, ...* → *Next, ...* / *Then, ...* → *After that, ...* → *Finally, ...*

## Modals of obligation, prohibition & advice

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`must` / `have to`** | Obligation (required rule) | *You **must** include a Primary Key.* |
| **`mustn't`** | **Prohibition** (forbidden—do NOT do it!) | *You **mustn't** share your password.* |
| **`don't have to`** | **No obligation** (optional—you can if you want) | *You **don't have to** print the code; PDF is fine.* |
| **`should` / `shouldn't`** | Advice / recommendation | *You **should** back up your project daily.* |

## Reading: Setting Up a Safe Home Wi-Fi Router

Read this original passage (~255 words) and answer the questions:

> Setting up a new home Wi-Fi router only takes ten minutes if you follow a clear order. First, connect the ISP modem cable to the router's **WAN** (or Internet) port and plug in the power adapter. Wait one minute until the power and internet lights stop blinking. Next, connect your laptop to the router with an Ethernet cable or select the default Wi-Fi name printed on the bottom sticker.
>
> Then, open a web browser and visit the router's admin address, usually `192.168.1.1` or `192.168.0.1`. You must change the default admin password immediately because hackers know factory passwords for every popular router brand. After that, open the Wireless Security menu and choose **WPA2-AES** or **WPA3**. You mustn't leave your network open or use old WEP encryption, which can be broken in minutes. Create a strong Wi-Fi password with at least twelve characters.
>
> You don't have to hide your Wi-Fi network name (SSID), because hiding it does not stop packet sniffers and only makes connecting your own phone harder. However, you should turn on automatic firmware updates so security patches install safely. Finally, click **Save** and wait for the router to reboot before testing a website.

? Why **must** you change the router's default admin password immediately?
- Otherwise the router will not turn on ~~ it turns on with factory settings
* Because factory default passwords for router brands are widely known to attackers ~~ easy target
- Because `192.168.1.1` stops working ~~ the IP stays the same
- Because WPA3 deletes passwords ~~ unrelated
! Default admin passwords are public knowledge and must be changed right away.

? What does the author say about **hiding your SSID**?
- You mustn't show your SSID ~~ opposite
* You don't have to hide it—it is optional and doesn't stop sniffers ~~ real security comes from WPA2/WPA3

## How a "how-to" web article is built

Websites are full of short articles such as *"How to save money as a student"*. They follow a clear pattern:

1. **A catchy title** with *How to…* or a number: *7 Easy Ways to Spend Less on Food*.
2. **A short introduction** (2–3 sentences) that shows the reader's problem: *Is your money gone by the 20th of every month? You're not alone.*
3. **Numbered steps or tips**, each with a **bold mini-heading** and 1–3 sentences. Use imperatives (*Make a list*), modals (*You should… / You don't have to…*) and a reason (*because…, so that…*).
4. **A friendly conclusion** with one last piece of advice: *Start small — even 50 lira a week adds up.*

Web articles speak directly to the reader (*you*), use short paragraphs and avoid long, complex sentences.

## Try it

Write a **150–180 word** web article: **"How to make your monthly student budget last."** Include a catchy title, an introduction, at least **four** numbered tips with mini-headings, and a conclusion. Use **imperatives**, **sequence linkers** and at least **three modals** (*must, mustn't, should, don't have to, have to*).

:::reveal Show model answer
**5 Simple Ways to Make Your Student Budget Last All Month**

Does your money disappear before the month ends? Don't worry — a few small habits can make a big difference.

**1. Write a budget first.** At the start of every month, write down your income and your fixed costs, such as rent and transport. You must know these numbers before you spend anything.

**2. Cook at home.** Eating out every day is expensive. You don't have to become a chef; simple meals like pasta or lentil soup are cheap and healthy.

**3. Use student discounts.** Always carry your student card. Many cinemas, museums and transport passes are cheaper for students.

**4. Wait 24 hours before big purchases.** You mustn't buy something expensive just because it is on sale. If you still want it the next day, then buy it.

**5. Save something — even a little.** Finally, put a small amount into savings as soon as you get your money. You should treat it like a bill.

Start with one tip this week. Small changes add up!
:::

- Catchy title and a short introduction that names the reader's problem
- At least four numbered tips with bold mini-headings
- Imperatives in each tip and at least three modals used correctly (*mustn't* = forbidden, *don't have to* = not necessary)
- Speaks directly to the reader (*you*); short paragraphs
- Friendly conclusion; 150–180 words

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **`mustn't`** (strictly forbidden!) with **`don't have to`** (optional / not necessary!).
- Using `must` for past obligation (`Yesterday I must study` → `Yesterday I had to study`).
- Using `-ing` in instructions instead of base imperatives (`Clicking Save` → `Click Save`).

## Key terms

- imperative | base verb form used for commands and step-by-step instructions | emir kipi
- prohibition (`mustn't`) | modal meaning something is forbidden | yasaklama
- lack of necessity (`don't have to`) | modal meaning something is optional | gereksizlik

## Quick check

? Choose the right modal: *"Entry is free today, so you ___ buy a ticket."*
- mustn't ~~ `mustn't` means buying a ticket is forbidden!
* don't have to ~~ buying a ticket is not necessary
- shouldn't ~~ illogical
- have to ~~ contradicts "free"
! Use `don't have to` when something is unnecessary.

? Choose the right modal: *"In a C exam, you ___ use your phone; anyone caught using one fails."*
- don't have to ~~ `don't have to` means phones are optional!
* mustn't ~~ strictly prohibited
- shouldn't ~~ too weak for a strict exam ban
- can ~~ opposite
! Use `mustn't` for strict prohibition.

? Which sentence is a **past** obligation?
- Yesterday I must finish my lab. ~~ `must` has no past form
* Yesterday I had to finish my lab. ~~ past of `must` / `have to` is `had to`
- Yesterday I have to finish my lab. ~~ present form doesn't match `Yesterday`
- Yesterday I musted finish my lab. ~~ `musted` does not exist
! Always use `had to` for past obligation.
