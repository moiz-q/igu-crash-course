You already study in English, so this page is not about everything — just the **grammar points the written exams test again and again**, and one simple plan for writing. Each idea has one example to copy.

:::idea Telling stories about the past
- **Past simple** — the main events: *She **opened** the door.*
- **Past continuous** — the background: *It **was raining**.*
- **Past perfect** — something **earlier**: *Someone **had left** a note.*

> *It **was raining** when she **opened** the door. Someone **had left** a note.*

[Go deeper: narrative tenses](#/lesson/en-02)
:::

:::idea Talking about the future
- **will** — predictions and quick decisions: *Prices **will** rise.*
- **going to** — plans, or something you can see coming: *I'**m going to** save money.*
- **present continuous** — arrangements: *I'**m meeting** my tutor on Friday.*
- **will be + -ing** — in progress at a future time; **will have + V3** — finished **by** a time: *By June I **will have finished**.*

[Go deeper: future forms](#/lesson/en-07)
:::

:::idea Passive: when the action matters more than the person
**be + past participle**: *Plastic **was invented** in 1907. Bottles **are recycled** here. Bags **have been banned**.*
Use it for processes, history and formal writing.

[Go deeper: passive](#/lesson/en-09)
:::

:::idea Joining ideas: relative clauses and guesses
- **who** (people), **which** (things), **where** (places), **whose** (possession): *The student **whose** laptop broke…*
- With commas, **never** use *that*: *Istanbul, **which** is huge, …*
- Guessing about the past: **must have** (sure), **might have** (maybe), **can't have** (sure it didn't): *He **must have missed** the bus.*

[Go deeper: relative clauses](#/lesson/en-08) · [past modals](#/lesson/en-12)
:::

:::idea One plan for every paragraph
1. **Topic sentence** — your main point.
2. **2–3 supporting points**, each with an example. Link them: *First, … In addition, … However, … For example, …*
3. **Concluding sentence** — *In short, … / On balance, …*

For comparisons use *both, whereas, unlike, in contrast*. For opinions: *In my opinion…, I agree up to a point, but…*

[Go deeper: comparison paragraph](#/lesson/en-14) · [advantages and disadvantages](#/lesson/en-10)
:::

:::remember If you remember only this
- Story = past simple (events) + past continuous (background) + past perfect (earlier).
- Future: will / going to / present continuous; *by* + future perfect.
- Passive = be + V3.
- No *that* after a comma; *must have / can't have* for past guesses.
- Paragraph = topic sentence → supported points with linkers → conclusion.
:::

## Quick check

? "When I arrived, the lecture ___."
- already started ~~ it happened before you arrived
* had already started ~~ past perfect for the earlier event
- has already started ~~ doesn't fit with *arrived*
- was already start ~~ wrong form
! Earlier past → past perfect.

? "By 2030, I ___ my degree."
- will finish ~~ *by* + future time needs future perfect
* will have finished ~~ finished before 2030
- am finishing ~~ an arrangement
- finished ~~ past
! *by* + time → will have + V3.

? Choose the passive: "They built the bridge in 1973."
* The bridge was built in 1973. ~~ was + V3
- The bridge built in 1973. ~~ *was* is missing
- The bridge has built in 1973. ~~ not passive
- The bridge is build in 1973. ~~ wrong tense and form
! Passive = be + past participle.

? Choose the correct sentence.
- Ankara, that is the capital, is big. ~~ no *that* after a comma
* Ankara, which is the capital, is big. ~~ correct
- Ankara, who is the capital, is big. ~~ *who* is for people
- Ankara which is the capital, is big. ~~ missing comma
! Comma + which (things) / who (people).

? "The streets are wet. It ___ last night."
* must have rained ~~ a strong guess from evidence
- can't have rained ~~ the evidence says it did
- must rain ~~ not past
- mustn't have rained ~~ not used for guesses
! Evidence → must have + V3.
