The vocabulary of the internet is full of **compound nouns**: *password, search engine, web page, data centre*. This lesson shows how English joins nouns together, when to use **'s** instead, and how to write a balanced paragraph about advantages and disadvantages.

## Compound nouns

A compound noun = **noun + noun** (sometimes adjective + noun). The **first** noun works like an adjective; the **second** noun is the main thing.

- a **search engine** = an engine (tool) for searching
- a **phone charger** = a charger for a phone (not a phone!)
- a **data centre** = a centre that holds data

They are written in three ways — you must learn each one:

| One word | Two words | Hyphen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| password, website, keyboard, smartphone, download | search engine, web page, home page, email address, data centre, credit card | e-book, log-in (noun), Wi-Fi, set-up |

### Grammar rules

1. The **first noun is usually singular**, even when the meaning is plural: *a **shoe** shop* (not *shoes shop*), *a **ten-minute** video*, *a **five-year** plan*.
2. The **plural** goes on the **second** noun: *two **search engines**, three **web pages***.
3. The **stress** is usually on the **first** part: **PASS**word, **SEARCH** engine.

## Possessive 's or compound noun?

| Use **'s** for… | Example |
| --- | --- |
| people and animals (possession) | *my **sister's** laptop*, *the **cat's** bowl* |
| time expressions | ***today's** news*, ***a week's** holiday* |
| organisations / places (often) | *the **university's** website*, ***Istanbul's** bridges* |

| Use a **compound noun** for… | Example |
| --- | --- |
| types / purpose of things | *a **computer** screen*, *a **coffee** cup* (a cup for coffee) |
| things that belong to things | *the **door** handle* (not *the door's handle*) |

Spelling: singular **'s** (*the student's bag* = one student); regular plural **s'** (*the students' bags* = many students); irregular plural **'s** (*the children's books*, *people's opinions*).

## Writing: an advantages-and-disadvantages paragraph

1. **Topic sentence** — introduce the topic and say there are two sides.
2. **Advantages** — 2 points, each with an example. *One advantage of… is that… · Another benefit is…*
3. **Disadvantages** — 2 points with examples. *On the other hand, … · A major drawback is… · However, …*
4. **Concluding sentence** — your balanced view. *Overall, … · On balance, …*

## Reading: Learning online — a balanced view (≈280 words)

> Over the last ten years, online learning has grown enormously. Today, a student in a small Anatolian town can watch a top university's lectures, join a programming course or practise English with a teacher in another country. But is learning online always a good thing?
>
> One clear advantage is **access**. Course materials, video lectures and e-books are available twenty-four hours a day, so students can study at their own speed. This is especially helpful for people with part-time jobs or family responsibilities. Another benefit is **cost**. Many online courses are free or much cheaper than classroom courses, and students save money on transport.
>
> On the other hand, online learning has some serious drawbacks. The first is **motivation**. Without a timetable or a teacher's face in front of them, many learners stop after a few weeks. In fact, completion rates for some free online courses are very low. A second problem is **the digital divide**: not everyone has a fast internet connection or a quiet room. A student whose family shares one smartphone is at a real disadvantage.
>
> Experts suggest that the best model is often a **mix**. Students attend lessons on campus for discussion, labs and teamwork, and use online resources for revision and extra practice. This "blended" approach keeps the human contact of the classroom while using the flexibility of the internet.
>
> On balance, online learning is a powerful tool, but it works best for learners who plan their time carefully — and it does not yet reach everyone equally.

? What is the writer's **overall** view?
- Online learning should replace universities ~~ the writer suggests a mix
* Online learning is useful but works best combined with classroom learning ~~ last two paragraphs
- Online learning is useless ~~ advantages are clearly listed
- Only rich students should learn online ~~ not stated
! The conclusion ("On balance…") gives the overall view.

? Find the **possessive**: which phrase shows possession with 's?
- e-books ~~ a compound noun
* a top university's lectures ~~ the lectures belong to the university
- video lectures ~~ a compound noun
- course materials ~~ a compound noun
! 's shows possession; noun + noun is a compound.

? What is "the digital divide" in the text?
- A type of software ~~ no
* The gap between people who have good internet access and those who don't ~~ paragraph 3
- A university department ~~ no
- A way to divide numbers ~~ no
! Use the context: "not everyone has a fast internet connection".

? Which phrase introduces the **disadvantages**?
- One clear advantage ~~ introduces advantages
- Another benefit ~~ advantages
* On the other hand ~~ signals the opposite side
- Experts suggest ~~ introduces the solution
! *On the other hand* contrasts two sides.

## Try it

Write a **120–150 word** paragraph: *"The advantages and disadvantages of social media for university students."* Use the four-part structure, at least **four compound nouns** and **two possessives**.

:::reveal Show model answer
Social media has become part of every student's daily routine, and it has both advantages and disadvantages. One advantage is that it makes communication easy. Class group chats help students share lecture notes, exam dates and project files in seconds. Another benefit is access to information: many professors' accounts and university pages post useful news and job adverts. On the other hand, social media can be a serious distraction. A quick look at a video app can easily become an hour, especially during exam weeks. A further drawback is privacy, because personal photos and phone numbers can be seen by strangers. On balance, social media is a useful study tool, but students should turn off notifications while they study and check their privacy settings.
:::

:::rubric Paragraph self-check
- Topic sentence that mentions both sides
- Two advantages and two disadvantages, each with an example
- Linking phrases: *One advantage…, Another…, On the other hand…, A further drawback…, On balance…*
- At least four compound nouns and two possessives, spelt correctly
- 120–150 words
:::

## Common mistakes

- **"a shoes shop", "a ten-minutes video"** → the first noun stays singular: *a shoe shop, a ten-minute video*.
- **"the computer's screen"** for things → *the computer screen*.
- Apostrophe in the wrong place: *the students's bags* → *the students' bags*.
- Using *its* and *it's*: **its** = possessive (*its screen*), **it's** = it is.

## Key terms

- compound noun | two or more words used together as one noun (search engine) | birleşik isim
- possessive 's | an ending that shows something belongs to someone | iyelik eki ('s)
- advantage / drawback | a good point / a bad point | avantaj / dezavantaj
- digital divide | the gap between people with and without good internet access | dijital uçurum
- blended learning | a mix of classroom and online learning | karma (harmanlanmış) öğrenme

## Quick check

? Which is correct?
- a five-years plan ~~ the number + noun is singular before another noun
* a five-year plan ~~ correct
- a five-year's plan ~~ no apostrophe
- a plan five years ~~ wrong word order
! In compounds, the first part is singular.

? "The ___ toys are in the garden." (toys belonging to several children)
- childrens' ~~ *children* is already plural; add 's
* children's ~~ irregular plural + 's
- childrens ~~ apostrophe missing
- children ~~ possessive needed
! Irregular plurals take 's: *children's, people's, women's*.

? In "phone charger", the main thing is:
* the charger ~~ the second noun is the main noun
- the phone ~~ the first noun describes the second
- both equally ~~ no
- neither ~~ no
! A phone charger is a kind of charger.
