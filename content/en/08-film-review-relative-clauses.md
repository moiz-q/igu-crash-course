Film reviews pack a lot of information into each sentence: who the characters are, where the story happens and what the reviewer thinks. **Relative clauses** make this possible. This lesson revises them and adds a more formal pattern: a **preposition + which/whom**.

## Quick review: relative pronouns

- **who** — people · **which** — things · **that** — people or things (defining clauses only) · **where** — places · **whose** — possession · **when** — times
- **Defining** clauses (no commas) tell us *which one*: *The actor **who plays the father** is excellent.*
- **Non-defining** clauses (with commas) add extra information: *The film, **which lasts three hours**, never feels long.* Never use *that* after a comma.

## Relative clauses with prepositions

Many verbs and adjectives go with a preposition: *live **in***, *talk **about***, *fall in love **with***, *be famous **for***, *depend **on***. When this verb is inside a relative clause, the preposition can go in **two places**:

| Formal (written reviews, essays) | Informal (speaking) |
| --- | --- |
| The town **in which** the story takes place is tiny. | The town **which/that** the story takes place **in** is tiny. |
| The woman **with whom** he falls in love is a spy. | The woman **who** he falls in love **with** is a spy. |
| The prize **for which** the film was nominated… | The prize **that** the film was nominated **for**… |
| The director, **about whom** little is known, … | *(non-defining: use the formal form in writing)* |

Rules:

1. After a preposition use **which** (things) or **whom** (people). **Never** *in that* or *with who*.
2. *in which / at which* can often be replaced by **where** for places: *the town where the story takes place*.
3. *in which / on which* can be replaced by **when** for times: *the year in which / when the war ended*.
4. Quantity words: *several characters, **most of whom** are teenagers* · *five songs, **all of which** are original*.

## Worked example — from two sentences to one

| Two sentences | One sentence (formal) |
| --- | --- |
| The film is set in a village. The village has no electricity. | The village **in which** the film is set has no electricity. |
| She works with a detective. The detective is new to the city. | The detective **with whom** she works is new to the city. |
| The film has eight characters. Most of them are strangers. | The film has eight characters, **most of whom** are strangers. |

## Practice (16 items)

Complete with **which**, **whom**, **where**, **when** or a **preposition**.

_ The house in ___ they grew up is now a museum. = which
_ The friend with ___ she travels is a photographer. = whom
_ The scene ___ which the two brothers argue is the best part. = in
_ The director, about ___ many books have been written, was born in Bursa. = whom
_ The year in ___ the film is set is 1923. = which
_ The city ___ the story takes place is never named. = where | in which
_ It is a story ___ which everyone can relate. = to
_ The film has five main characters, all of ___ are students. = whom
_ The soundtrack contains twelve songs, most of ___ are original. = which
_ The prize for ___ the actor was nominated is the most important in Europe. = which
_ The day ___ the film opened, the cinema was full. = when | on which
_ The character ___ I felt most sorry for was the old teacher. = who | whom | that
_ The problem ___ which the hero must deal is his own fear. = with
_ That is the actress ___ brother directed the film. = whose
_ The book on ___ the film is based sold millions of copies. = which
_ The island to ___ they escape looks peaceful but hides a secret. = which

## Reading: a film review (≈300 words)

> **"The Last Signal" — ★★★★☆**
>
> *The Last Signal*, which opened in cinemas across Türkiye last week, is a quiet but powerful science-mystery film. It tells the story of Leyla, a radio engineer **with whom** the audience immediately sympathises. She lives alone in a mountain station **in which** old equipment is used to listen for signals from space.
>
> One winter night, Leyla receives a short message in a language that nobody can identify. The scientists **to whom** she sends the recording first think it is a joke. Only her former teacher, Professor Aydın, **whose** career was destroyed by a similar discovery thirty years earlier, takes her seriously.
>
> The film's greatest strength is its atmosphere. The station, **where** most of the action happens, feels both cosy and frightening. The director uses long, silent shots **during which** we hear only the wind and the static of the radio. Some viewers may find these scenes slow, but they create real tension.
>
> The acting is excellent. Deniz Kaya, **for whom** this is a first leading role, gives Leyla a quiet strength. The supporting cast, **most of whom** appear in only one or two scenes, are also convincing.
>
> The film is not perfect. The final twenty minutes, **in which** the mystery is explained, rely too much on dialogue, and the ending will divide audiences. However, this is a thoughtful, beautifully filmed story **that** asks a simple question: if someone out there called us, would anyone listen?
>
> **Recommended for:** viewers who enjoy slow, intelligent mysteries rather than action.

? What is the reviewer's **overall** opinion?
- Very negative ~~ four stars and mostly positive comments
* Mostly positive, with a weaker ending ~~ praises atmosphere and acting; criticises the last 20 minutes
- The film is only for children ~~ not stated
- The reviewer did not finish the film ~~ the review describes the ending part
! Stars + the last paragraph usually show the overall view.

? Who believes Leyla at first?
- All the scientists ~~ they think it's a joke
* Professor Aydın, her former teacher ~~ paragraph 2
- Deniz Kaya ~~ that's the actress
- The audience ~~ not the point here
! Detail question — scan for "takes her seriously".

? In "long, silent shots **during which** we hear only the wind", *which* refers to:
- the wind ~~ no
* the shots ~~ we hear the wind during the shots
- the radio ~~ no
- the station ~~ no
! The relative pronoun refers to the noun just before the clause.

? What does the reviewer criticise?
- The acting ~~ praised
- The atmosphere ~~ praised
* The last twenty minutes, which rely too much on dialogue ~~ paragraph 5
- The length of the title ~~ not mentioned
! Criticism is signalled by "not perfect" and "However".

## Try it

Write a **100–130 word** review of a film or series you know. Include: what it is about, one strength, one weakness, and who you recommend it to. Use at least **two relative clauses with a preposition** (*in which*, *with whom*, *most of whom*…).

:::reveal Show model answer
*Kış Uykusu* is a slow drama set in a small hotel in Cappadocia, in which a retired actor lives with his wife and sister. The film follows long conversations during which old family problems slowly come to the surface. Its biggest strength is the dialogue: the characters, none of whom are completely good or bad, feel very real. The landscape, which looks beautiful in the snow, also reflects the cold relationships. However, at more than three hours, the film is too long for many viewers, and some scenes repeat the same arguments. I would recommend it to people who enjoy thoughtful films and do not mind a slow pace.
:::

:::rubric Review self-check
- Says what the film is about in 1–2 sentences (no big spoilers)
- Gives one strength and one weakness, with a reason
- Uses at least two relative clauses with a preposition, correctly (*which/whom*, never *that/who* after a preposition)
- Ends with a recommendation; 100–130 words
:::

## Common mistakes

- **"the city in that she lives"** → *the city **in which** she lives* (no *that* after a preposition).
- **"the man with who she works"** → *with **whom***.
- Using the preposition twice: *the town **in** which she lives **in*** → remove one.
- Forgetting commas in non-defining clauses: *The director, who is only 30, …*

## Key terms

- review | an article that describes and judges a film, book, etc. | eleştiri, inceleme yazısı
- plot | the story of a film or book | olay örgüsü
- relative clause | a part of a sentence that describes a noun, starting with who/which/that… | sıfat cümleciği (ilgi cümleciği)
- supporting cast | actors in the smaller roles | yardımcı oyuncular
- spoiler | information that tells you the end of a story too early | sürprizbozan

## Quick check

? Choose the correct formal sentence.
- The hotel in that we stayed was quiet. ~~ *that* can't follow a preposition
* The hotel in which we stayed was quiet. ~~ preposition + which
- The hotel in which we stayed in was quiet. ~~ preposition used twice
- The hotel in where we stayed was quiet. ~~ no preposition before *where*
! Preposition + *which* (things) / *whom* (people).

? "The students, ___ of whom had seen the film, discussed it in class."
- that ~~ not a quantity word
* most ~~ *most of whom* = most of the students
- who ~~ *who of whom* is wrong
- which ~~ wrong
! Quantity word + *of whom/which*.

? Which word can replace *in which* in "the village **in which** the film is set"?
- when ~~ for times
- whose ~~ for possession
* where ~~ for places
- that ~~ *that* would need *in* at the end
! *in which* (place) = *where*.
