When we describe how something **is made**, what **was invented** or what **has been done** about a problem, the important thing is usually the **action**, not the person who did it. That is when English uses the **passive voice**. Our topic is plastic: its history and its uses.

## Active vs. passive

> **Active:** Factories **produce** about 400 million tonnes of plastic every year.
> **Passive:** About 400 million tonnes of plastic **are produced** every year.

Passive = **be** (in the right tense) + **past participle (V3)**. Add **by + agent** only if the agent is important: *Bakelite **was invented by** Leo Baekeland.*

| Tense | Passive form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present simple | am/is/are + V3 | Plastic bottles **are made** from PET. |
| Past simple | was/were + V3 | The first fully synthetic plastic **was created** in 1907. |
| Present perfect | has/have been + V3 | Plastic bags **have been banned** in many countries. |
| Present continuous | am/is/are being + V3 | New materials **are being tested**. |
| Future (will) | will be + V3 | More plastic **will be recycled** in the future. |
| Modal | can/must/should be + V3 | Bottles **can be recycled** many times. |

### When do we use the passive?

- The agent is unknown or obvious: *My bike **was stolen**.*
- We describe a **process** (science, technology, factories): *The plastic **is melted** and **poured** into a mould.*
- Formal and academic writing.

## Giving a short talk (2–3 minutes)

A good short talk has a clear structure. Use **signposting** phrases so listeners know where you are:

| Part | Useful phrases |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | *Today I'd like to talk about… · I've divided my talk into three parts.* |
| Moving on | *First of all, … · Let's move on to… · Turning now to…* |
| Examples | *For example, … · A good example of this is…* |
| Conclusion | *To sum up, … · So, in conclusion, … · Thank you for listening. Are there any questions?* |

Speaking tips: look up from your notes, speak a little more slowly than normal, and pause after each main point.

## Reading: a short talk about plastic (transcript, ≈310 words)

> "Good afternoon, everyone. Today I'd like to talk about a material **that is used** by almost every person on Earth every single day: plastic. I've divided my talk into three parts: its history, its uses, and its problems.
>
> First of all, history. Natural plastic-like materials, such as rubber, **have been used** for centuries. However, the first fully synthetic plastic, Bakelite, **was invented** in 1907 by a Belgian-American chemist. It **was used** for telephones and radios because it did not melt when it got hot. After the Second World War, mass production began, and cheap plastic products **were sold** all over the world.
>
> Let's move on to uses. Plastic is light, strong and cheap. Medical equipment such as syringes **is made** from plastic because it can **be thrown** away after one use, which keeps patients safe. Cars **are made** lighter with plastic parts, so they use less fuel. And of course, almost all of our phones and laptops **are covered** in plastic.
>
> Turning now to the problems. The biggest one is waste. Only a small part of all plastic **has ever been recycled**. The rest **is burned**, **buried** or ends up in nature. Very small pieces, called microplastics, **have been found** in seas, in fish and even in drinking water.
>
> So what **is being done**? Single-use bags **have been banned** or **charged** for in many countries, including Türkiye. New materials made from plants **are being developed**. But scientists say that the most effective action is the simplest one: use less, and reuse more.
>
> To sum up, plastic changed modern life, and in many ways it made life safer and easier. But if nothing **is changed**, the problems **will be passed** on to the next generation. Thank you for listening. Are there any questions?"

- **Intro:** Today I'd like to talk about glass bottles. I've divided my talk into three parts: how they are made, how they are used, and how they are recycled.
- **Part 1:** Glass **is made** from sand, soda and limestone. The mixture **is heated** to about 1,500 °C and **is blown** into moulds.
- **Part 2:** Moving on to uses — drinks, medicines and food **are stored** in glass because it doesn't change the taste.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting **be**: *The bottle made in Germany* → *The bottle **was** made…*
- Wrong participle: *was builded* → *was **built***; *was choosed* → *was **chosen***.
- Using the passive with verbs that have no object: *The accident **was happened*** → *The accident **happened***.
- Adding *by people / by someone* — it adds nothing. Leave it out.

## Key terms

- passive voice | a structure (be + V3) where the action is more important than who does it | edilgen çatı
- agent | the person or thing that does the action (by …) | eylemi yapan (fail)
- signposting | phrases that show the structure of a talk | yönlendirme ifadeleri
- recycle | to process used material so it can be used again | geri dönüştürmek
- single-use | made to be used once and thrown away | tek kullanımlık

## Quick check

? Choose the passive form: "They **are building** a new recycling plant."
- A new recycling plant is built. ~~ wrong tense (present simple)
* A new recycling plant is being built. ~~ present continuous passive
- A new recycling plant has built. ~~ not passive
- A new recycling plant was being build. ~~ wrong tense and wrong participle
! Present continuous passive: am/is/are **being** + V3.

? Which sentence is **wrong**?
- The bottles are collected on Monday. ~~ correct
* The meeting was happened yesterday. ~~ *happen* has no object, so no passive
- Paper can be recycled. ~~ correct
- The law has been changed. ~~ correct
! Only verbs with an object can be passive.

? When is the passive most useful?
- When the agent is the most important information ~~ then use active
* When describing a process, or when the agent is unknown or obvious ~~ correct
- Only in questions ~~ no
- Only in the past ~~ all tenses have a passive
! Passive focuses on the action or the thing affected.
