Two high-frequency grammar points in English I are **sense verbs** (`look`, `sound`, `feel`, `taste`, `smell`) and **articles** (`a`, `an`, `the`, or no article).

## Verbs of the senses

| Pattern | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sense verb + adjective` | Describes a quality | *Your code **looks clean**.* / *That idea **sounds great**!* |
| `sense verb + like + noun` | Compares to a noun | *This fabric **feels like** silk.* / *It **sounds like** a fan problem.* |

*(Remember: it is **sound** (by ear) vs. **feel** (by touch/emotion), and never put an adverb like `cleanly` after `look/smell/taste` when describing the subject!)*

## Articles: `a / an / the / Ø`

| Article | When to use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`a` / `an`** | First mention of a singular countable noun (`a` before consonant **sound**, `an` before vowel **sound**) | ***a** university* (`/juː/` = consonant sound!), ***an** hour* (silent `h`!), ***an** SQL query* (`/ɛs/`) |
| **`the`** | Specific/known item, second mention, unique things, superlatives | *Open **the** file I emailed you.* / ***The** sun* / ***the** best* |
| **Zero (`Ø`)** | General plural nouns, general uncountable nouns, most countries/languages/meals | ***Computers** use binary.* / ***Water** is essential.* / *I study **English**.* |

## Practice: Gap-fill (15 items)

_ That new keyboard ___ (feel) really comfortable. = feels ~~ `feel + adjective`
_ Your laptop fan sounds ___ a tiny airplane! = like ~~ `sound like + noun`
_ This coffee tastes ___ (bitter) without sugar. = bitter ~~ `taste + adjective`
_ She waited for ___ hour outside the exam hall. = an ~~ `hour` starts with a vowel sound
_ Istanbul Gelisim is ___ university in Avcilar. = a ~~ `university` starts with `/juː/` consonant sound
_ Please turn off ___ projector when you leave Room 204. = the ~~ specific projector in Room 204
_ ___ (Ø / The) programmers need patience when debugging. = Ø ~~ plural noun in general
_ I bought a mouse and a cable; ___ mouse is wireless. = the ~~ second mention
_ Did you see ___ moon last night? = the ~~ unique object
_ He speaks ___ (Ø / the) Turkish and English fluently. = Ø ~~ languages take zero article
_ Can you send me ___ email with the PDF attached? = an ~~ `email` starts with vowel sound
_ That song sounds ___ (wonderful)! = wonderful ~~ `sound + adjective`
_ My little brother looks ___ our father. = like ~~ `look like + noun`
_ ___ (Ø / The) honesty is important in teamwork. = Ø ~~ abstract uncountable noun in general
_ She is ___ (a / the) fastest typist in our class. = the ~~ superlative takes `the`

## Reading: How Sensors Give Machines "Senses"

Read this original passage (~260 words) and answer the questions:

> Humans rely on five senses—sight, hearing, touch, taste, and smell—to understand the world. Today, engineers build electronic **sensors** that give computers and robots similar abilities. A smartphone camera acts like an eye, a microphone acts like an ear, and a touchscreen feels the tiny electrical charge of your finger.
>
> In modern factories and smart homes, sense-like sensors prevent costly accidents. Gas detectors can "smell" a dangerous leak long before a human nose notices anything unusual. When a machine's motor wears out, a vibration sensor detects that the motor no longer sounds smooth; instead, it sounds like metal scraping against metal, triggering an automatic maintenance alert. Even agricultural drones use special cameras to check whether crops look healthy or need water.
>
> Choosing the right sensor requires careful engineering. Just as a chef tastes soup to adjust the salt, a food-processing plant uses chemical sensors to test acidity and sweetness automatically. Without these devices, self-driving cars would be blind, and hospital monitors could not track a patient's heartbeat. Sensors turn physical feelings, sounds, and light into binary numbers that a C or Python program can read and act upon in milliseconds.

? How does a vibration sensor help prevent machine damage in a factory?
- It smells gas leaks ~~ gas detectors smell leaks
* It detects abnormal vibrations when a motor stops running smoothly and sends an alert ~~ catches wear before breakdown
- It waters crops automatically ~~ drones check crop health
- It replaces the factory's electricity ~~ unrelated
! Vibration sensors detect unusual motion/noise early and trigger maintenance alerts.

? Why does the passage compare a camera to an eye and a microphone to an ear?
- Because cameras and microphones are made of biological cells ~~ they are electronic devices
* Because electronic sensors convert light and sound into data, just as human senses gather information ~~ helps explain what sensors do
- Because computers have emotions ~~ computers process binary numbers
- Because humans cannot hear or see ~~ humans use both
! Sensors perform the electronic equivalent of human sensory input.

## Try it

Write a short description (80–100 words) of **your favorite café, library, or study spot**. Use **three different sense verbs** (at least one with `+ adjective` and one with `+ like + noun`) and check every **`a / an / the`** carefully.

:::reveal Show model answer
My favorite study spot is a quiet corner on the second floor of the university library. The room always smells like fresh paper and mild coffee from the hallway café. Soft afternoon light makes the wooden desks look warm and inviting, and the chairs feel comfortable even during a two-hour coding session. When the campus gets busy, the library still sounds peaceful—almost like a gentle rain outside. It is the best place to focus before an exam.
:::

- Uses at least 3 sense verbs (`smells like`, `look warm`, `feel comfortable`, `sounds peaceful`)

## Common mistakes

- Writing `an university` or `a hour`. Always choose `a` vs. `an` by **sound**, not spelling (`a university`, `an hour`, `an MP3`).
- Saying `Your idea sounds like great` (`like` is ONLY for nouns; use `sounds great` for adjectives!).
- Putting `the` before general plural or uncountable nouns (`The computers are useful` → `Computers are useful`).

## Key terms

- sense verb | verb describing perception (`look, sound, feel, taste, smell`) | duyu fiili
- definite article (`the`) | article used for specific, known, or unique nouns | belirli tanımlık
- zero article (`Ø`) | omitting `a/an/the` before general plurals or uncountable nouns | tanımlıksız kullanım

## Quick check

? Choose the **correct** phrase:
- an university ~~ `university` starts with `/j/` consonant sound → `a university`
* an honest answer ~~ `h` in `honest` is silent, so it starts with a vowel sound → `an`
- a hour ~~ `h` in `hour` is silent → `an hour`
- a MP3 file ~~ `M` is pronounced `/ɛm/` → `an MP3 file`
! Choose `a` vs. `an` by the first **sound** of the word.

? Choose the **correct** sentence:
- That pizza smells like delicious! ~~ `delicious` is an adjective, so drop `like`
* That cloud looks like a dragon! ~~ `a dragon` is a noun, so `looks like` is correct
- He felt happily after passing the exam. ~~ use adjective `happy`, not adverb `happily`
- This music sounds terribly. ~~ use adjective `terrible`
! Use `sense verb + adjective`, or `sense verb + like + noun`.

? Choose the **correct** article: *"___ coffee in this thermos is still hot, even though I normally don't drink ___ coffee."*
- Ø / the ~~ first is specific (`the coffee in this thermos`); second is general (`Ø`)
* The / Ø ~~ specific thermos coffee (`The`); general uncountable coffee (`Ø`)
- A / a ~~ `coffee` as a substance is uncountable here
- The / the ~~ second mention is general habit, not specific
! Specific known noun = `the`; uncountable noun in general = zero article (`Ø`).
