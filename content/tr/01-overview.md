## 1. Language and Turkish among world languages

**Language** is a living, rule-governed system that people use to share thoughts and feelings. It changes slowly over time and carries culture.

Linguists group world languages in two ways:
- **By origin (language families):** Turkish belongs to the **Ural–Altaic** (or **Altaic / Turkic**) family, alongside Mongolian and Tungusic languages (some linguists classify Turkic as its own primary family). It is **not** related to Arabic (Afro-Asiatic) or English/Persian (Indo-European), even though it borrowed words from them.
- **By structure:** Turkish is an **agglutinative** (*eklemeli*) language. You build new words and grammatical forms by adding suffixes (*ek*) to an unchanging root (*kök*), like snapping Lego blocks together:
  - *ev* (house) → *ev-ler* (houses) → *ev-ler-imiz* (our houses) → *ev-ler-imiz-de* (in our houses).

## 2. Historical periods of Turkish

1. **Old Turkish (*Eski Türkçe*, 6th–13th c.):** Orkhon Inscriptions (*Orhun Yazıtları*, 8th c.), Uyghur texts.
2. **Middle Turkish (*Orta Türkçe*, 13th–15th c.):** Karakhanid, Khwarazmian, Old Anatolian Turkish.
3. **Ottoman Turkish (*Osmanlı Türkçesi*, 15th–20th c.):** Heavy Arabic and Persian vocabulary in literature and administration.
4. **Modern Turkish (*Türkiye Türkçesi*, 1928–present):** The Latin alphabet reform (1928) and language simplification.

## 3. Sounds and vowel harmony (*Ünlü Uyumu*)

Modern Turkish has **29 letters**: **8 vowels** and **21 consonants**.

The 8 vowels are grouped by three features:
- **Back (*kalın*) vs Front (*ince*):** `a, ı, o, u` (back) vs `e, i, ö, ü` (front)
- **Unrounded (*düz*) vs Rounded (*yuvarlak*):** `a, e, ı, i` (unrounded) vs `o, ö, u, ü` (rounded)
- **Wide (*geniş*) vs Narrow (*dar*):** `a, e, o, ö` (wide) vs `ı, i, u, ü` (narrow)

| Rule | How it works | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Great Vowel Harmony** (*Büyük Ünlü Uyumu*, front/back) | If the first vowel is **back** (`a, ı, o, u`), the following vowels are **back**. If **front** (`e, i, ö, ü`), they are **front**. | *kapı-lar* (doors), *ev-ler* (houses), *okul-da* (at school) |
| **Small Vowel Harmony** (*Küçük Ünlü Uyumu*, rounding) | After **unrounded** (`a, e, ı, i`) comes unrounded (`a, e, ı, i`). After **rounded** (`o, ö, u, ü`) comes **wide unrounded** (`a, e`) or **narrow rounded** (`u, ü`). Note: `o, ö` never appear in non-first syllables in native words. | *göz-lük-ler* (glasses), *kol-um-da* (on my arm) |

:::tip Consonant changes
- **Consonant softening (*ünsüz yumuşaması*):** `p, ç, t, k` at the end of a word soften to `b, c, d, ğ` before a vowel suffix: *kitap + ı → kitabı*, *ağaç + a → ağaca*.
- **Consonant assimilation (*ünsüz benzeşmesi*):** After voiceless `f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p` (*Fıstıkçı Şahap*), suffixes starting with `c, d, g` harden to `ç, t, k`: *mutfak + da → mutfakta*, *kitap + cı → kitapçı*.
:::

## 4. Word structure and word types

- **Root (*kök*):** The smallest meaningful base (*göz*, *gel-*).
- **Derivational suffix (*yapım eki*):** Changes the meaning or word type (*göz → göz-lük*, *göz-lük-çü*).
- **Inflectional suffix (*çekim eki*):** Shows plural, case, possession, tense or person without making a new dictionary word (*ev-ler-de*).

The **8 word types** (*sözcük türleri*):
1. **Noun (*isim*):** names things (*masa, sevgi*).
2. **Adjective (*sıfat*):** describes a noun (*kırmızı araba*).
3. **Pronoun (*zamir*):** replaces a noun (*ben, şu, kim*).
4. **Adverb (*zarf*):** modifies a verb, adjective or adverb (*hızlı koştu*, *dün geldi*).
5. **Postposition (*edat*):** shows relation after a word (*senin gibi*, *sabaha kadar*).
6. **Conjunction (*bağlaç*):** joins words or clauses (*ve, ama, çünkü*).
7. **Interjection (*ünlem*):** expresses emotion or call (*eyvah!, hey!*).
8. **Verb (*fiil*):** shows action or state (*yazmak, uyumak*).

## 5. Verbs, moods, verbals and sentence elements

- **Indicative tenses (*haber kipleri*):** `-yor` (present continuous), `-r / -ar / -er` (aorist/simple present), `-di` (seen past), `-miş` (heard/reported past), `-ecek` (future).
- **Moods (*dilek kipleri*):** `-meli` (necessity), `-se` (conditional), `-e` (optative), imperative (*gel!*).
- **Verbals (*fiilimsiler*):** Words formed from verbs that act like nouns (*isim-fiil*: `-me, -mek, -iş`), adjectives (*sıfat-fiil*: `-an, -ecek, -miş, -dik`), or adverbs (*zarf-fiil*: `-ip, -erek, -ince`).
- **Sentence elements (*cümlenin ögeleri*):**
  - **Predicate (*yüklem*):** the core verb or nominal predicate (usually at the end in Turkish).
  - **Subject (*özne*):** who/what does the action.
  - **Object (*nesne*):** definite (`-i`) or indefinite (*ne?*).
  - **Adverbial (*zarf tümleci*)** and **Indirect object (*dolaylı tümleç*):** answer where, when, how, why.

## Key terms
- agglutinative | building words by adding suffixes to a root | eklemeli
- vowel harmony | matching suffix vowels to the stem's last vowel | ünlü uyumu
- root | the smallest meaningful base of a word | kök
- derivational suffix | a suffix that creates a new word or word class | yapım eki
- inflectional suffix | a suffix that marks grammar (plural, case, tense) | çekim eki
- predicate | the main verb or statement word of a sentence | yüklem
