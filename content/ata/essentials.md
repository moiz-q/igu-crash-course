This course tells **one story**: how the Ottoman Empire weakened, was defeated in the First World War, and how a national movement led by Mustafa Kemal won independence. Learn the four big steps and a few dates — the full timeline has everything else.

:::idea A weakening empire tries to reform (1700s–1913)
The empire fell behind Europe in science, industry and the army. Reformers tried to save it: **Selim III** and **Mahmud II** modernised the army; the **Tanzimat Edict (1839)** promised equal rights; the **First Constitution (1876)** created a parliament. Thinkers argued about the way forward: **Ottomanism, Islamism, Turkism, Westernism**. The **Balkan Wars (1912–13)** still cost almost all European lands.
:::

:::idea Defeat and occupation (1914–1919)
The empire joined **World War I** on the losing side. Mustafa Kemal became famous defending **Çanakkale (Gallipoli, 1915)**. The **Armistice of Mudros (30 Oct 1918)** let the Allies occupy the country, and Greek forces landed at **İzmir (15 May 1919)**.
:::

:::idea Organising the resistance (1919–1920)
**19 May 1919:** Mustafa Kemal lands at **Samsun** — the start of the National Struggle. The **Amasya Circular** said "the nation's independence will be saved by the nation's own will". The **Erzurum** and **Sivas Congresses** united the resistance; the **National Pact (Misak-ı Millî)** set the goals. On **23 April 1920** the **Grand National Assembly (TBMM)** opened in Ankara. The Istanbul government signed the **Treaty of Sèvres (1920)**, which would have divided Anatolia — Ankara rejected it.
:::

:::idea Victory and a new state (1921–1923)
The army won at **Sakarya (1921)** and broke the Greek lines in the **Great Offensive (26 Aug 1922)**, reaching İzmir on 9 September. The **Armistice of Mudanya (1922)** ended the fighting, and the **Treaty of Lausanne (24 July 1923)** recognised Turkey's full independence and borders. (The Republic was declared on 29 October 1923 — that is covered in Atatürk II.)
