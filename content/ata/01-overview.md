This is a concise overview of **Atatürk Principles and History of Revolutions I (ATA101E)**, a compulsory 2-ECTS course. It covers the late Ottoman reform efforts, the collapse of the empire after World War I, the National Struggle led by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, and the diplomatic recognition of the new Turkish state in 1923.

## Big-picture summary

1. **Why reform failed to save the Empire:** From Selim III and Mahmud II through the Tanzimat (1839) and the two Constitutional Eras (1876, 1908), Ottoman leaders tried administrative, legal and military reforms. Loss of the Balkans (1912–13) and defeat in World War I (1914–18) left the empire shattered.
2. **From occupation to organization (1919–1920):** After the Armistice of Mudros (1918) and the occupation of İzmir (May 1919), Mustafa Kemal landed at Samsun (19 May 1919). The Amasya Circular, Erzurum Congress and Sivas Congress built a unified national movement. When Allies occupied Istanbul (March 1920), the Grand National Assembly (TBMM) opened in Ankara (23 April 1920).
3. **Military victory and diplomacy (1920–1923):** After securing the Eastern and Southern fronts, the regular army stopped the Greek advance at Sakarya (1921) and won decisively in the Great Offensive (August–September 1922). The Armistice of Mudanya (October 1922) and the **Treaty of Lausanne** (24 July 1923) established Turkey's internationally recognized independence.

## Key terms
- Tanzimat | 1839 reform edict promising legal equality and fairer taxation | Tanzimat Fermanı
- Armistice of Mudros | 30 Oct 1918 truce ending WWI for the Ottoman Empire | Mondros Ateşkes Antlaşması
- National Pact | 1920 declaration defining Turkey's borders and independence | Misak-ı Millî
- Grand National Assembly | Turkish parliament opened in Ankara on 23 April 1920 | Türkiye Büyük Millet Meclisi (TBMM)
- capitulations | special legal and economic privileges given to foreign states | kapitülasyonlar
- Treaty of Lausanne | 24 July 1923 peace treaty recognizing Turkish sovereignty | Lozan Barış Antlaşması
